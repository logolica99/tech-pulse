Tesla has announced Powercore, a medium-voltage power electronics unit that puts a solid-state transformer, an inverter and switchgear with a circuit breaker into one enclosure. The company is showing it this week at RE+ 2026 in Las Vegas.

## The specs

According to Tesla's figures, reported by pv magazine USA and Electrek, Powercore delivers up to 7.4 MW (9 MVA) continuously at 34.5 kV AC, with up to 10 MVA of peak power for grid-forming. It works with 800 V to 2,000 V on the DC side and 10 kV to 34.5 kV on the AC side. Tesla lists a peak efficiency of 98.86%, a warranty of up to 25 years, and an operating range of −40°C to 55°C.

Tesla says the built-in solid-state breaker can clear faults in about 100 microseconds, which it describes as roughly 1,000 times faster than conventional protection. Electrek notes the unit uses no flammable oil and can be serviced in the field module by module. Tesla says it will be made in the US, with first units operating in 2027. It has not shared pricing, customers, or a factory location.

## What it is for

Tesla is pitching one product for several markets:

- **Solar:** it takes DC straight from arrays, supporting today's 1,500 V plants and coming 2,000 V designs, so developers don't need separate inverters and transformers.
- **Storage:** it connects to Megapack batteries over a DC bus and can arrive pre-assembled. Tesla says this replaces a two-step setup that had twice the losses.
- **AI data centers:** it converts grid power to 800 V DC for server racks in a single step. Tesla says it is working with Nvidia on 800 V DC designs and on certification for Nvidia's DSX Ready program.

The bigger pitch is a way around conventional transformers, where lead times have stretched to as long as four years as solar, storage and data center projects compete for equipment.

## The competition

Electrek points out that Powercore lands in the same year that Heron Power, a startup founded by former Tesla energy and powertrain chief Drew Baglino, plans volume production of its own solid-state transformer, Heron Link. On paper, Tesla's unit has a higher power rating. Heron, though, already has named customers, a planned pilot site, and a factory under construction in Morgan Hill, California.
