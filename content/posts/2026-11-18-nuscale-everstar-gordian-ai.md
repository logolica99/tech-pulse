NuScale Power, the small modular reactor (SMR) developer, said on November 18 that it is deploying Gordian, a nuclear-specific AI platform from New York startup Everstar, across the teams working to bring its reactors to market.

## From pilot to rollout

NuScale says an initial pilot produced efficiency gains, so it is now extending Gordian into licensing, plant services, project management, supply chain, quality assurance and product development. Nuclear projects run on huge volumes of regulatory and engineering documents, and that is the work Gordian targets: it applies nuclear regulatory frameworks across connected document sets to help assess how an engineering change might ripple through, and pairs repeatable workflows with a natural-language interface.

NuScale co-founder and CTO José Reyes said the tool takes on time-consuming tasks so engineers can focus on decisions, with the goal of shorter timelines and lower costs. The two companies say they are evaluating further uses. These efficiency claims come from the companies themselves; no figures were given.

## Context

NuScale's design is the only SMR with a design certified by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, according to the company. Each NuScale Power Module produces 77 megawatts of electricity, and plants can scale to 12 modules (924 MW). Everstar says it is also moving into robotics and streamlined qualification to speed up reactor fabrication.
