XPENG used its eighth AI Day in Guangzhou on November 25 to give its humanoid robot IRON its first public showing, alongside a new AI architecture it plans to share across cars and robots.

## IRON

According to XPENG, IRON has 76 degrees of freedom and more than 1,500 tactile sensing points covering over 80% of key body areas, and runs on three of the company's own Turing AI chips. It supports autonomous navigation, tasks given by voice or app, self-charging and fault reporting. IRON is set to start training as a store assistant in XPENG shops in Q1 2027, with a public release targeted for Q2 2027.

To back it up, XPENG says it has built a robot data factory covering more than 250 scenarios and 15 job categories, plus an automated assembly line with over 1,000 tests for finished robots and key parts.

## Turing Physical AI Architecture

The new architecture, also called VLA 3.0, links three layers: a very fast layer for body control, a fast layer running its VLA 2.0 model for real-time reactions, and a slower layer for planning complex tasks. XPENG says the latest VLA 2.0 version can drive its own closed-loop training, speeding training 10 to 12 times.

## Cars too

The company also said its first purpose-built robotaxi, YOYO-01, is due in 2027, and that a new adaptive deceleration feature increased usable range 12.4% in its urban congestion tests, with an over-the-air rollout planned for December 2026.
