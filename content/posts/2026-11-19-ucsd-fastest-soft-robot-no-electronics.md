Engineers at the University of California San Diego have built what they describe as the fastest robot made entirely of soft materials. The six-legged machine reaches up to 4.72 meters per second, a little over five yards a second, and it has no electronics at all. The work appears in the Nov. 18 issue of *Science Robotics*.

## Borrowing from the steam engine

The robot is 3D-printed in one piece from a single material. Inside is a network of chambers, valves and tubes that drives the legs when pressurized air flows through. The team's earlier air-powered walker from 2022 managed only about 4 centimeters per second, limited by how much pressure and airflow it could handle.

To go faster, the researchers made the robot and its internal structures larger and stronger so they could take higher pressure. But at high pressure the robot wasted a lot of energy in its exhaust air. Their fix borrows from the compound steam engine: air first runs through small high-pressure chambers that drive the valves, then through larger low-pressure chambers that move the legs. The result is more than 100 times faster than the 2022 version.

## Where it can go

In tests the robot ran over concrete, gravel, lawn, mulch and sand, went down stairs, swam, and kept working underwater. For now it needs a tether to an air compressor. The team, led by Yichen Zhai and Professor Michael T. Tolley, plans to try designs that use less air so the robot could carry a portable source such as a compressed-air cartridge, and to add steering and object handling.

Because these robots don't rely on electronics, the researchers see uses in places where electronics struggle, such as high-radiation or strong magnetic environments, disaster response and space exploration. The National Science Foundation funded the work.
