Nvidia reported results for its fiscal third quarter after the market closed on Tuesday, the first time it has done so since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week. The numbers did not show a slowdown.

## The quarter

For the three months ended October 25, 2026, Nvidia said revenue reached $115.8 billion, up 103% from $57.0 billion a year earlier and up 20% from the prior quarter. Data center sales made up almost all of it at $107.6 billion, up 110% year over year. Net income was $65.3 billion, and adjusted earnings came in at $2.68 per share.

According to LSEG figures cited by CNBC, analysts had expected $109.1 billion in revenue and $2.48 in adjusted earnings per share. Nvidia also said its Vera Rubin NVL72 systems have started shipping.

## The outlook

Nvidia guided to about $130 billion in revenue for the fourth quarter, plus or minus 3%, a period that includes an extra week. That is above the roughly $125 billion analysts expected, per CNBC. The company again said it is assuming no data center compute revenue from China.

On the call, interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress said Nvidia now expects 75% growth in its next fiscal year, up from an earlier 70% forecast, and described the situation inside the company as "business as usual." Board chair Harvey Jones opened the call by saying the board does not plan to reinstate Huang and would not take questions about his resignation or the ongoing investigation.

CNBC reported that the stock rose more than 3% in after-hours trading, after falling 24% so far this month.
