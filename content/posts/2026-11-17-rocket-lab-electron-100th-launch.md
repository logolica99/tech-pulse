Rocket Lab's small Electron rocket has now flown 100 times. The milestone mission, called "The Mountain God Rises," lifted off from Rocket Lab Launch Complex 1 in New Zealand on November 14 and placed the YAMATSUMI-II synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imaging satellite into a roughly 575 km low Earth orbit for Japanese operator iQPS.

Rocket Lab says Electron got to 100 launches faster than any other commercially developed rocket, less than a decade after its first flight in 2017. SpaceNews reported the launch as reaching that milestone faster than any other commercial rocket.

## A steady customer

This was Rocket Lab's 11th Electron launch for iQPS since 2023, all of them successful according to the company, and iQPS has booked seven more dedicated Electron flights through 2030 to finish building out its radar constellation. iQPS reported making first contact with the new satellite shortly after deployment.

## What comes next

Rocket Lab CEO Peter Beck said in the company's announcement that "the next 100 Electrons are already booked." Electron flies from pads in New Zealand and Virginia, and Rocket Lab describes it as the operational foundation for its upcoming reusable medium-lift rocket, Neutron.
