Japanese construction firm Shimizu Corporation says it has built a one-piece, 1-meter-class rocket propellant tank using metal 3D printing, which it describes as among the largest such tanks made this way in Japan. An internal burst test confirmed the tank withstood pressure up to 1.7 MPa.

## Two robots, one tank

The tank came out of a large hybrid additive manufacturing system that pairs two robotic arms. One deposits the structure layer by layer by melting metal wire (wire arc additive manufacturing). The other measures and machines it: a laser profiler on that arm tracks the build's shape in real time, and the data feeds back to adjust later deposition paths and layer heights. When the shape drifts, printing pauses, the machining robot fixes the defect, and printing resumes.

## Where it's headed

The system grew out of joint research with JAXA that began in 2022. Since 2024, Shimizu has continued work on large liquid propellant tanks under Japan's Space Strategy Fund. The company says it plans to use the measurement and pressure-test data to refine the process, with a goal of printing 2.5-meter-class tanks suited to small and medium-lift rockets by fiscal year 2028.

Shimizu frames the effort as part of a global push to cut launch costs as demand for rocket launches grows.
