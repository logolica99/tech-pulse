Chinese robot maker AgiBot confirmed on November 25 that its first batch of G2 humanoid robots has gone into round-the-clock work at JCET's automotive electronics factory in Shanghai's Lingang area. JCET is one of the world's largest chip packaging and testing companies.

## The job

The robots load and unload trays, sort chips and separate defective units at stations built for human workers. AgiBot says each cycle takes under 25 seconds, chips are placed with 0.7 mm accuracy, and good and bad chips are told apart with a 99.999% success rate. Those figures come from the company.

The G2 has seven-degree-of-freedom arms and a three-degree-of-freedom waist, which gives it the reach to work existing trays and stations.

## Why it stands out

Most public humanoid deployments so far involve moving boxes or restocking shelves. Chip packaging asks for sub-millimeter precision and near-zero sorting errors, which makes it a tougher test. AgiBot has not said how many robots are deployed or what the deal is worth.
