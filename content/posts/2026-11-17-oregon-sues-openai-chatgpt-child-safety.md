Oregon has become the latest state to take OpenAI to court. On Tuesday, Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed a lawsuit in Multnomah County Circuit Court seeking at least $2.035 billion. It accuses the company of putting children at risk and misleading parents about how safe ChatGPT is.

## What the state alleges

The complaint relies mainly on Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act. The state says OpenAI misrepresented ChatGPT as safe for kids, sold a product with serious safety problems it did not disclose, and used unfair tactics to market to children and teens. It also brings claims of negligence, unjust enrichment and public nuisance.

The state's release cites studies showing ChatGPT has exposed minors to harmful content about self-harm, eating disorders and suicide. It also says the chatbot's human-like design can make vulnerable kids emotionally dependent on it. OPB reports that the 53-page complaint says OpenAI often fails to confirm that a parent approved accounts for users under 18. The complaint also argues that the safeguards in the newer "ChatGPT for Teens" version are easy to get around and don't alert parents when a teen asks about topics like suicide or self-harm.

Oregon wants the court to stop OpenAI from making misleading safety claims and to require protections that actually work.

## OpenAI's response and context

An OpenAI spokesperson told OPB the company is committed to AI policies "that apply to the entire AI industry and not just one company." The statement closely matched earlier ones in which OpenAI pointed to parental controls, break reminders and age-appropriate model protections.

Rayfield told OPB that no single Oregon incident prompted the suit. According to OPB, eight other states sued OpenAI in October over similar concerns about teens. The filing is part of a new Oregon DOJ "Keeping Kids Safe" initiative that also includes a $125 million settlement with Meta and an ongoing investigation into Discord.
