**Short answer: $3.79 billion in retail sales through the end of launch day, and 32.5 million units sold before release. Those are official figures from Take-Two and Rockstar, published on Friday, November 20.**

## What changed

Yesterday the only number available was an outside estimate. Sensor Tower put launch-day sales at about 24.6 million copies and about $2.36 billion, and Take-Two had not said anything (see [our earlier post](/posts/2026-11-19-gta-6-day-one-sales)). Now the publisher has released its own figures, and they are much higher.

## What Take-Two announced

In a press release, Take-Two said:

- GTA 6's **day-one retail sales reached $3.79 billion**. Take-Two calls it a new record for entertainment in any medium and the biggest entertainment launch in history.
- The game **sold 32.5 million units pre-release**, which Take-Two says is a record across all platforms.
- Both numbers are **company estimates for worldwide sales through the end of the day on November 19**, launch day.

The release also mentions the game's 99 score on Metacritic. Rockstar founder Sam Houser said the studio was "incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world."

Note that the 32.5 million figure is the number of units sold before release. Take-Two did not give a separate count of copies sold on launch day itself.

## How it compares

Polygon and GameSpot point out that GTA 5 made about $800 million on its first day in 2013 and reached $1 billion after three days. GameSpot also notes that GTA 5 launched at $60, while the cheapest version of GTA 6 costs $80.

## Official or not?

- **Official, from Take-Two and Rockstar:** $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales and 32.5 million units sold pre-release, both based on the company's own estimates through the end of November 19.
- **Not official:** Sensor Tower's earlier estimate of about 24.6 million copies and $2.36 billion was a third-party guess, and it turned out to be well below the company's numbers. Comparisons such as "bigger than any movie's whole run" are outlets' own math, not Take-Two figures.
