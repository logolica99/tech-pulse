California's Department of Motor Vehicles has stopped Waymo from operating or testing its driverless vehicles on the state's freeways. The order took effect on November 11 but only became public on Tuesday.

## Why the DMV acted

The DMV told TechCrunch and KQED that it considers Waymo's driverless freeway operations an "imminent hazard to public safety." It pointed to vehicles repeatedly entering active construction zones where workers and equipment were present. Last week, three Waymo vehicles drove into an active construction closure on U.S. Route 101 near Burlingame. In a separate incident, a Waymo vehicle appeared to try to get around a California Highway Patrol traffic break.

The regulator said these incidents followed earlier restrictions and fixes made after similar construction-zone incidents on May 18, 2026. The new restriction stays in place until Waymo shows it has taken effective corrective action, including evidence that its cars can reliably detect and respond to construction zones and emergency vehicles.

## Waymo's response

Waymo said none of the recent incidents caused injuries or collisions. Beyond complying in California, it has temporarily stopped freeway rides in all of its other markets while it evaluates performance. Its robotaxis keep running on surface streets, and the company said it serves more than half a million trips a week. KQED reported that Waymo will keep testing on freeways with trained autonomous specialists while the restriction is in place.

## Why it matters

Freeway rides launched in late 2025 and have been important to Waymo's expansion, especially in the Bay Area, where its service area runs from San Francisco to San José. Freeways cut travel times on longer trips and to airports. This is the second time this year Waymo has stopped freeway driving. It paused in May, then came back in July after a software update meant to handle construction zones better.
