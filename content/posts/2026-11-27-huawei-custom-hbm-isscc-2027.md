Huawei is preparing to show its own high-bandwidth memory (HBM) design at the International Solid-State Circuits Conference (ISSCC) in San Francisco in February. HBM is the stacked memory that sits next to AI accelerators, and the market is currently dominated by Samsung and SK hynix.

## What the program lists

According to the ISSCC 2027 preliminary program, as reported by TrendForce and Aju Press, Huawei's paper describes:

- **64 channels and a 4,096-bit data interface**, twice the 2,048-bit width of the HBM4 standard.
- **Memory controllers integrated** with the stacked memory.
- **Five-layer wafer-on-wafer hybrid bonding**, which joins whole wafers rather than individual dies. It allows dense vertical connections but demands very precise alignment and uniform bonding.

The presentation is scheduled for February 16, one of four Huawei papers at the conference.

## What it does not say

Aju Press notes the program entry gives no capacity, speed, yield or production timeline, and a wider interface alone does not prove higher bandwidth. For comparison, Samsung's HBM4 delivers up to 3.3 TB/s per stack, and Samsung will present a 48GB HBM4E design at the same event.

TrendForce reports Huawei already uses self-developed HBM in its Ascend 950 accelerators. Chinese memory maker CXMT will also present a DDR5 design built with wafer-on-wafer hybrid bonding. A Seoul National University professor quoted by Sisa Journal, via TrendForce, said limited access to advanced chipmaking tools is pushing Chinese firms toward such 3D integration approaches.
