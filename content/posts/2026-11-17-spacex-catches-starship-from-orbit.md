SpaceX has caught a Starship second stage coming back from orbit. On November 16, the 52-meter vehicle known as Ship 42 lowered itself into the giant mechanical arms on the launch tower at Starbase, Texas, about 22.5 hours after it left the same pad.

The tower arms had caught Super Heavy boosters three times before, but those were coming back from short suborbital climbs. This was the first catch of an upper stage returning from orbital speed.

## The flight

This was Starship's 15th full-scale flight test and its longest so far, beating a roughly three-hour orbital flight in September. Over 14 orbits the ship deployed 26 next-generation Starlink satellites, which SpaceX said were healthy and raising their orbits.

To come home, the ship fired one engine for about 10 seconds to start its descent, did its belly-flop maneuver through the atmosphere, then flipped upright and lit its engines one last time to settle into the arms.

Not everything went to plan. A day earlier the company called off the attempt to catch the booster just minutes after liftoff, after onboard systems flagged a fuel-system alarm, and the booster splashed down offshore instead. SpaceX says it is still investigating the cause.

## Why it matters

Catching both stages on a routine basis is how SpaceX wants to make Starship a fully reusable orbital rocket. That would speed up Starlink deployment, and Starship is also the planned lander for NASA's crewed Moon missions, which could start as early as 2028. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman congratulated the team on X.

SpaceX will now study the flight data, including how well Ship 42's heat shield held up on reentry. It is also preparing Starship operations at Kennedy Space Center in Florida, with another launch site planned in Louisiana.
