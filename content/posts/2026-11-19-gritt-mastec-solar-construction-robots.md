MasTec, one of North America's largest infrastructure contractors, has agreed to use robots from startup Gritt on its renewable energy jobsites. The rollout begins with MasTec's utility-scale solar portfolio across multiple states. The companies also plan to look at other infrastructure sectors.

## Robots that bolt onto existing machines

Gritt doesn't build new vehicles. It makes robotic attachments for equipment contractors already own, such as skid steers and forklifts. Its AI learns physical jobs like picking and placing, assembly and moving materials, while craft workers guide the system and check its work. The company says the system also monitors jobsite activity to help with planning. Gritt says its systems have worked in rain-soaked fields, deserts and snowy plains.

The deal follows testing on live jobs. Gritt says MasTec Renewables evaluated its systems while installing megawatts of solar panels on active sites. Rob Turner, MasTec Renewables' vice president of construction, described the technology as support for MasTec's crews, not a replacement.

## Who is Gritt

Gritt is based in South San Francisco. According to Construction Dive, it launched in July with $32.4 million in combined seed and Series A funding. Its backers include Obvious Ventures, Union Square Ventures, First Round Capital, Climactic and Congruent Ventures. Construction Dive notes that the deal follows other big bets on construction robotics, including Bedrock Robotics' roughly $600 million round in late October.
