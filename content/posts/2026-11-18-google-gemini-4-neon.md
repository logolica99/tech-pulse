Google has added a new member to its Gemini 4 family. Gemini 4 Neon is meant to deliver close to top-tier reasoning while running faster and cheaper than the company's largest model, Gemini 4 Carbon.

## Where it fits

Google describes the lineup this way: Carbon handles the hardest long-horizon projects, Hydrogen covers fast everyday tasks, and Neon sits in the middle for complex but high-volume work like coding, analysis, and enterprise workflows. It has four thinking levels so teams can trade intelligence for token spend.

## Price and availability

Neon costs $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 for cached input) and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1 million-token context window. It is rolling out in the Gemini API through Google AI Studio, in Gemini for business, in Google Antigravity for paid AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, and in the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers.

## Google's benchmark claims

According to Google, Neon scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its highest thinking level, placing third overall, and ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index. Google also says it ranks second on the Vals Index and on Arena's new Agent Arena Index, and that at its default setting it costs 32% less per task than Carbon. These are the company's own figures.

## Cyber and safety

A cybersecurity-focused variant, Neon for cyber, will reach vetted defenders over the coming days through Google's Fairwind Program. Google says Neon keeps Carbon-level protections against misuse and prompt injection while refusing far fewer legitimate requests.
