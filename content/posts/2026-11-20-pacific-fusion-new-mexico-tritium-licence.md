Fusion startup Pacific Fusion has cleared a key regulatory step for its New Mexico campus. The New Mexico Environment Department approved a radioactive materials licence that lets the company possess and use tritium, a radioactive form of hydrogen used as fusion fuel, at its Research and Manufacturing Campus in Albuquerque, World Nuclear News reports.

Pacific Fusion says it is the first such licence the department has ever issued to a private fusion company. The company says the small amounts of tritium involved will stay inside a closed system, and any waste will go to a licensed facility outside the state.

## What it's building

The California-based company broke ground in August on the $1 billion campus at Mesa del Sol. It will house Pacific Fusion's Demonstration System, which the company says is designed to reach net facility gain by 2030, meaning it would release more fusion energy than the total energy first stored in the machine.

The system is also meant to produce fusion bursts above 100 megajoules. Pacific Fusion describes it as the only "high-yield" fusion facility under construction in the US, which would give researchers a way to study conditions relevant to maintaining the nuclear stockpile without explosive testing.

Founded in 2023, Pacific Fusion is developing a pulser-driven inertial fusion approach built from modular, mass-producible parts, drawing on decades of work at Lawrence Livermore, Sandia and Los Alamos national laboratories. The licence comes about a year after the company chose New Mexico.
