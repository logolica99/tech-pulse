Australian energy company AGL says the first 100 units of its Tomago battery are now in place. The 500 MW, 2,000 MWh system is being built in the Tomago Industrial Estate north-west of Newcastle, New South Wales, and will plug into the National Electricity Market that serves Australia's eastern and southern states.

## Progress on site

Concrete foundations are finished, and the 100 units installed so far are part of more than 400 that will make up the battery. According to AGL, work on the substation is well advanced, with the first of two main transformers delivered last week, while crews lay cabling and install inverters. More than 200 people are working on the project, which broke ground in December 2025 and is still on track for completion in late 2027.

## Grid-forming at scale

Fluence is supplying its Gridstack Pro system and is also building and maintaining the project, which it called its biggest deal globally when AGL took the final investment decision in July 2025. Tomago uses grid-forming inverters, which can provide system strength to the grid without the spinning machines found in coal and gas plants.

The project costs about AU$800 million (roughly US$538 million) and won underwriting support from the NSW government's Long-Term Energy Service Agreement scheme in May 2026.
