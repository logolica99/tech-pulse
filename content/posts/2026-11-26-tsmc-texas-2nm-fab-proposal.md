TSMC is weighing Texas for its next big US chip plant. Documents filed with the Texas Comptroller's Office show the company seeking an estimated $354 million property tax break for a proposed $22.6 billion manufacturing facility in Brazos County, Manufacturing Dive reports.

## The proposal

- The site would cover more than 3,200 acres and include at least one fab making 2-nanometer chips for AI, high-performance computing and mobile devices.
- TSMC says the project would create at least 2,099 direct jobs by 2032 once fully operational, and produce $64.4 billion in economic output over 40 years.
- The tax break would come through Texas's Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation (JETI) program. TSMC called it a "determinative and compelling factor" in choosing the state.

## Not settled yet

TSMC says it is also considering expanding in Arizona instead, where it already has fabs and cites advantages in workforce, logistics, suppliers and existing government relationships. The College Station school board will hold a public meeting on December 10, and both the school district and the Texas governor must approve the application before a tax agreement can be signed.

The proposal follows TSMC's broader US build-out. Manufacturing Dive notes the company's US pledges across 2024 to 2026 total $265 billion, funding 12 planned facilities.
