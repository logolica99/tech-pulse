South Korean Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigned on Sunday, November 22, taking responsibility for the confusion caused by rushed rules meant to stop AI-assisted cheating on the national college entrance exam, the Suneung. Yonhap reports that President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation about an hour after Choi announced it.

"As the minister in charge, I believe I cannot escape criticism that we failed to prepare in advance," Choi said in a statement quoted by Yonhap.

## What set it off

The BBC reports that fears of AI cheating spiked after the top scorer on April's national tax accountancy exam admitted he had photographed questions with a tiny camera, sent them to an AI tool and read the answers on his phone. Officials then introduced emergency measures in the days before the Suneung, which was held on Thursday and taken by about 500,000 students.

The rules included letting candidates bring only pencils into exam rooms and covering clothing buttons with stickers to block hidden cameras. Authorities also sent three emergency disaster alerts to every mobile phone in the country during the exam to expose candidates who had not handed in their devices. The BBC says it was the first time the national alert system was used to catch cheaters.

The education ministry said 111 candidates were found with unauthorised devices. Students and teachers complained that the late changes added stress to an exam that weighs heavily on careers in South Korea. Lee has asked the prime minister to handle follow-up measures, and a successor will be nominated through the usual process.
