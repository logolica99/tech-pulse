**Short answer: in the early chapters, ambush other crooks and take their loot, and sell older cars to Moses. From Chapter 3, rob jewelry stores through the back door with the snap gun, sell stolen cars at a Pay 'n' Spray, and hit banks fast before the police arrive.** What works best depends on how far you are in the story.

## Early game (Chapters 1 and 2)

- **Rob other criminals.** You will often run into a crime already happening, like a bank job, a jewelry heist, an armored car being hit, or a getaway car fleeing police. PC Gamer's top tip is to take down that crew and grab their bags. It says this pays about $1,000 to $15,000, which beats most robberies you can pull off yourself in Chapters 2 and 3. Expect the heat to land on you, so leave quickly.
- **Sell cars to Moses.** The mechanic Moses arrives in Chapter 2, gives you a slim jim, and buys older, low-profile cars, usually for one to a few thousand dollars each. He sometimes tells you where the cars he wants are. After a sale there is a wait before he buys another.
- **Demolition derby.** PC Gamer says wins at Ambrosia Raceway pay about $3,800 to $6,300 and can be repeated. You win by being the last car still running, so crashing into everyone works.
- **Hunting.** Bret buys animal skins. PC Gamer got $2,500 for one large boar. Polygon notes that if you shoot a big animal (boar, bear or alligator) you can call Bret to collect it, while smaller kills you have to deliver yourself.

## Mid game (Chapter 3 onward)

- **Back-door jewelry stores.** The snap gun arrives in Chapter 3 and lets you pick locks without setting off alarms. Polygon puts a typical jewelry store take at about $20,000. Go in through a back door, knock out the guards, find the safe code in the back office (on a screen or a note), wipe the security footage and leave. Polygon names Victoria Lee Jeweler in Capri and Blanc and Giroux in the Decor District as good targets.
- **Pay 'n' Spray car sales.** A Pay 'n' Spray takes any car that has no advanced tracker. Polygon says stealing the most valuable car near the Ocean Beach shop gets about $7,000 for half a minute's work. Each shop has a short cooldown between sales.
- **Randy's cars.** Randy, the car fence from Chapter 3, pays far more than Moses, but he wants flashier, high-end cars.
- **Quick bank robberies.** Polygon says bank robberies open near the end of Chapter 3. Skip the vault, have your partner keep people under control while you empty the registers, and leave when you hear sirens. That gets about $15,000 per bank.
- **Pilot Pete's drops.** PC Gamer says the pilot Pete, met in Chapter 3, offers timed plane drop jobs in Chapter 4 that pay about $10,000 to $20,000 per run.

## Smaller earners

Mugging people, opening car trunks, rooftop lockboxes, diving for shipwreck treasure, races and other contests, and selling collectibles at pawn shops all pay, but both guides say they pay less or take longer. Once you have cash, bank it. Our guide to [banking and saving money in GTA 6](/posts/2026-11-19-gta-6-bank-save-money) explains how.

## Official or not?

Rockstar has not published an official money guide for GTA 6. Everything above comes from PC Gamer's and Polygon's own play, and the dollar amounts are their reported takes. Treat them as rough figures, not fixed payouts.
