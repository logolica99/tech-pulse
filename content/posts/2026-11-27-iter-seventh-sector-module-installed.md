ITER, the international fusion experiment being built in southern France, has installed the seventh of the nine sector modules that make up its tokamak's doughnut-shaped core. Only two of the 40-degree segments remain.

This is an update to our [earlier post on the sixth module](/posts/2026-11-19-iter-council-six-of-nine-vacuum-vessel-modules). What's new is both the count and the difficulty of the lift.

## Why this one was harder

In the six earlier installations there was always open space on at least one side of the module. This time, crews had to lower a five-storey-tall module into the gap between two modules already in place. The suspended load, including the lifting rig, weighed nearly 1,400 tonnes, with about five centimetres of clearance on each side. The operation took 24 hours over November 25 and 26.

Director-General Pietro Barabaschi said the lift "went exactly to plan" and that keeping pace as the work gets more complex gives the organization confidence in its schedule.

## Schedule

Sector module installation began in April 2025 and passed the two-thirds mark in July 2026. ITER says machine assembly is now running about six months ahead of the schedule set in its Baseline 2024 plan.
