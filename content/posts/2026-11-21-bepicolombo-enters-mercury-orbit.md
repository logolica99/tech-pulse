BepiColombo, the joint mission of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), is now in orbit around Mercury. ESA announced the orbit insertion on November 21, Nature reports. It is only the second mission to orbit the planet, after NASA's MESSENGER from 2011 to 2015.

## A long road in

The spacecraft launched on an Ariane 5 from French Guiana in October 2018. Reaching Mercury is hard because a probe falling toward the Sun picks up a lot of speed and has to shed it, so BepiColombo flew past Earth once, Venus twice and Mercury six times on the way, according to Space.com. A solar panel problem that cut power to its electric thrusters added about a year to the trip. The Guardian puts the mission's cost at about €1.6 billion.

## Two orbiters, two paths

BepiColombo carries two science probes: ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter, called Mio. They are still joined together. Per Nature, Mio should reach its own elliptical orbit, between about 500 km and 11,000 km above the planet, around December 9, where it will study Mercury's magnetic field and how it interacts with the solar wind. MPO will then lower itself step by step until March 2027, settling into an orbit between about 400 km and 1,500 km to map the surface.

## What comes next

Space.com reports that science operations are planned to begin on April 6, 2027. Researchers hope to learn how Mercury formed so close to the Sun, why it has a magnetic field and an unusually large iron core, and what makes up the ice in its permanently shadowed craters. One instrument, the Mercury Imaging X-ray Spectrometer, is the first X-ray imaging device sent on an interplanetary mission, Nature notes.
