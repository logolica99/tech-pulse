Japanese fusion startup Helical Fusion has been formally selected for a major government grant program run by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI). The maximum subsidy is about ¥19.2 billion, or roughly $126 million, and the supported project runs through February 2029.

## What the money pays for

The company says the grant will speed up three tracks of its Helix Program:

- **Helix HARUKA**, an integrated demonstration device meant to prove that key fusion technologies work together.
- **Helix KANATA**, the basic engineering design of its first fusion power plant.
- **Core technologies**, the critical components a commercial plant will need.

With this award, Helical Fusion says its total funding and financing commitments come to about ¥33.2 billion (around $218 million).

## Where the hardware stands

HARUKA is already being built at the National Institute for Fusion Science (NIFS) campus in Toki, Japan. According to the company, one helical coil is nearly assembled, and the first phase will test energizing its high-temperature superconducting coils in 2027. A second phase in the early 2030s is planned to show HTS coils and blanket systems operating together, lowering risk before a power plant is built. Design work on KANATA will run in parallel rather than waiting for every test to finish.

## Why a stellarator

Helical Fusion, a 2021 spin-out from NIFS research, says it picked the helical stellarator design because it can potentially deliver net electricity, run continuously, and be maintained efficiently. It points to NIFS's Large Helical Device, which has sustained plasma for 3,268 seconds. The company coordinates work with about 40 Japanese firms on coils, blankets, power systems, and structures, and is aiming for commercially viable fusion power in the 2030s.
