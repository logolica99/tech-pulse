NASA announced on November 17 that its Terra satellite will stop collecting science data on December 1, closing out a 27-year run for one of the agency's longest-serving Earth-observing spacecraft.

## Four times its design life

Terra was launched in December 1999 as a workhorse of NASA's Earth Observing System and was designed to last six years. NASA says it has now operated for more than four times that long, but its systems are aging and its orbit is drifting, which is why the agency is ending the mission.

## What it measured

The satellite carries five instruments: ASTER, CERES, MISR, MOPITT and MODIS. According to NASA, MOPITT and parts of CERES and ASTER had already been switched off to save power.

Over its lifetime, Terra built a long record of land-use change, wildfire behavior, the movement of dust and smoke, atmospheric composition and ice cover. NASA says that data has fed into decisions on public safety, agriculture and water resources, as well as commercial and humanitarian uses.

## Shutting down

After science operations end, the Terra team will use the spacecraft's remaining fuel to lower its orbit, then power off all of its systems in January 2027. NASA says Terra's data records will remain free and publicly available.
