Stoke Space has wrapped up development of Zenith, the methane-fueled engine that will lift its Nova rocket off the pad. The company announced the milestone in a social media post on Thursday, Ars Technica reports.

Stoke said that as flight engines pass acceptance testing, it is shipping them to Cape Canaveral every week to be integrated into Nova's first stage. Seven Zenith engines power that reusable booster, producing about 700,000 pounds of thrust together at full throttle.

## The harder part is upstairs

Stoke said earlier this year that Nova's first stage had passed a "proto-qualification" milestone. The second stage is now going through cryogenic proof tests and hot-fire testing at the company's test site in rural Washington.

That upper stage is what makes Nova unusual. It uses a ring of 24 thrusters around a regeneratively cooled heat shield that protects the stage as it comes back through the atmosphere. If it works, Stoke would be able to reuse both stages of the rocket.

Nova's first launch is currently expected sometime next year, according to Ars.
