The National Governors Association (NGA) on November 22 published a set of principles for governing AI at both the state and federal level. They come from its bipartisan Governors Council to Secure American AI Dominance, led by Maryland Gov. Wes Moore, Indiana Gov. Mike Braun, North Carolina Gov. Josh Stein and Utah Gov. Spencer Cox. The NGA says the goal is to line up state approaches and shape the AI framework the incoming Congress is expected to write.

## The six principles

- **Innovation and protection together.** AI policy should keep US technology leadership while protecting safety, civil rights and privacy, because public trust is part of winning the race.
- **Support workers.** States should treat job creation as a core goal and help workers whose jobs change because of AI.
- **Split the roles.** Washington should handle national and international AI risks, while states keep the flexibility to govern how AI is used within their own responsibilities.
- **Evidence-based safeguards.** Rules should use clear, technically informed definitions and proportionate safeguards, reviewed as the technology changes.
- **Consistency.** Federal and state rules should avoid needless duplication and conflicts without stripping out protections that work.
- **Competition.** Laws should not favor large companies or put up barriers for smaller entrants.

## The ask to Washington

In the release, the governors stress that a federal framework should not sideline states. Cox said many states already have policies that protect children and consumers while encouraging AI adoption. NGA CEO Brandon Tatum said no federal AI policy will work without governors' input and that any national framework should keep appropriate state authority.
