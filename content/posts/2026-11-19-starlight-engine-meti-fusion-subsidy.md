Japanese fusion startup Starlight Engine (SLE) says it has been formally selected for a subsidy from Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) under its fusion power generation demonstration program. The award is worth up to ¥23.3 billion and covers fiscal years 2026 through 2028. SLE had been named a conditional awardee in August.

It is the second Japanese fusion company in two days to confirm a METI award, after stellarator developer Helical Fusion.

## What the money is for

SLE will run the project with Kyoto Fusioneering, its group company. The work is titled a "High-Temperature Superconducting Tokamak Power Generation Demonstration Project" and spans ten technical areas, according to the company, including:

- **Coils:** building and testing high-temperature superconducting conductors and model coils.
- **Divertor and heating:** new divertor materials, long-pulse neutral beam injection, and high-power gyrotrons.
- **Fuel and blanket:** safe handling of tritium, blanket test modules, and secondary cooling and power generation systems.
- **Maintenance and materials:** a remote maintenance demonstrator, irradiation testing, and facilities for handling activated materials.
- **Control:** diagnostics plus machine-learning control models designed to cope with missing data.

The company says the goal is to prove out the parts that decide whether a plant can work commercially, such as a workable thermal cycle, maintainability, and plant availability, rather than just plasma physics.

## Where FAST stands

FAST (Fusion by Advanced Superconducting Tokamak) is a private-sector-led project that aims to produce and sustain a deuterium-tritium burning plasma and show an integrated power generation system in the 2030s. SLE says it has finished the conceptual design, is now in engineering design, and plans to start construction in 2028–2029.

SLE raised ¥6.06 billion in its first external funding round in July 2026. A call for a construction site closed on October 15 and drew applications from several local governments, which the company is now surveying. Partners listed on the project include Hitachi, Fujikura, Furukawa Electric, Mitsubishi Corporation, Mitsui & Co., Marubeni, J-POWER, and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation.
