China's People's Liberation Army is "actively soliciting" Nvidia's most advanced chips to build up its AI-enabled military capabilities, according to the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission's 2026 annual report to Congress, released on Wednesday, November 18.

The commission, set up by Congress in 2000 to track the security implications of U.S.-China economic ties, says Beijing is trying to evade U.S. export controls to get the semiconductors behind what the PLA calls "intelligentized warfare": weaving AI into weapons, command and control, kill chains and logistics, as Reuters summarized it.

## A persistent chip gap

The report says Chinese processors, including Huawei's Ascend and Cambricon's chips, still trail products from Nvidia and AMD. According to CNBC, the commission concluded that China has not yet closed the hardest chokepoints, naming advanced logic fabrication, memory, chipmaking equipment and Nvidia's CUDA software ecosystem, and likely won't for some time. It described the resulting compute gap as substantial, largely because of U.S. export controls.

The commission also pointed to a workaround: Chinese companies reaching Nvidia chips remotely through data centers in third countries such as Malaysia. The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued a rule this week aimed at closing that route.

## More pressure on Nvidia

The findings land as Nvidia faces mounting scrutiny over its China business after Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO amid reports linking him to a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed, a company U.S. investigators are examining for possible diversion of Nvidia chips to China. Quartz also notes earlier reports that a PLA-linked Chinese university bought two H200 chips in October despite being on a U.S. export blacklist.
