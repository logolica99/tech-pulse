The Council of the EU and the European Parliament have reached a provisional agreement on a permitting directive meant to speed up approvals for renewable energy projects, electricity grids, energy storage and EV charging stations. It is one half of the European grids package that the European Commission proposed in December 2025.

The Council says transmission grids currently take about 10 years to build, and permitting accounts for more than half of that time.

## Key changes

- **Deadlines.** Solar equipment and co-located storage (excluding hydrogen) get a maximum three-month permitting period, according to PV Tech. Standalone storage and EV charging stations get six months, and pumped hydro gets two years. Grid connection procedures face a three-month deadline, which can be extended to six months in justified cases.
- **Overriding public interest.** Renewables and grids will be presumed to be of overriding public interest. Member states can set that aside only to protect security and defence or cultural heritage.
- **Tacit approval.** Member states may treat a lack of response from authorities at intermediate steps as approval.
- **One digital portal.** Permit applications will go through single digital portals.
- **Smaller projects.** The size above which small solar, storage and charging installations need an administrative permit rises from 100 kW to 200 kW. Charging stations of 1 MW or less on artificial structures need no permit at all.
- **Benefit sharing.** Renewable projects above 7 MW, down from 10 MW, must share some of their economic benefits with local host communities. Projects run by renewable energy communities are exempt.

## What happens next

The deal still needs formal endorsement and adoption by both the Council and Parliament, starting with a vote in Parliament's industry and energy committee. After that, member states will have 18 months to write the rules into national law.
