Sila, the battery materials company founded in 2011, has signed an agreement to supply its silicon-carbon anode material to Panasonic Energy. Sila says the deal could be worth more than $1 billion over time. It is the company's first mass-market supply deal.

## Why silicon anodes matter

Today's lithium-ion batteries mostly use graphite anodes. According to Sila, its silicon-based anode weighs a fifth as much as graphite and takes half the volume, so a battery can store more energy in the same space and weight. The company also says the material supports more power and faster charging. For electric vehicles, that could mean longer range.

## Scaling up Moses Lake

Sila opened its factory in Moses Lake, Washington, in September 2025. CEO Gene Berdichevsky told MIT Technology Review that the Panasonic deal will help scale production to at least 10 times current levels. He said Panasonic has committed to buy tens of thousands of tons of material, but Sila is not sharing the total volume or price.

Part of the financing behind that production is a $1.4 billion loan the US Defense Department committed to Sila earlier this year. The factory also received $100 million from the Department of Energy in 2022.

## A rare bright spot

The deal lands after a hard stretch for next-generation batteries in the US. MIT Technology Review notes that two billion-dollar battery companies, 24M and ESS Tech, went bankrupt and shut down in the past year. It also points to a shift in how the industry talks about itself: Sila's announcement focuses on American manufacturing and reducing dependence on China, which controls over 90% of graphite production, rather than on climate.
