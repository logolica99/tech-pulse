Humyn Labs, a startup in Bengaluru, India, has raised $47 million in Series A financing to collect the real-world data robots need to learn physical tasks. The round includes $26 million in equity led by Accel, with Prosus Ventures, Jump Trading and others joining, plus $21 million in venture debt led by Lighthouse Canton with Trifecta Capital.

## Recording real work

The company built its own two-camera hardware to film people doing everyday work and household chores. Software then tracks hand movements and object interactions and labels the footage so AI models can learn from it. Humyn Labs collects data in more than 20 countries across Latin America and Asia, with Brazil, India and South Korea as its main hubs. Co-founder Manish Agarwal told TechCrunch it records people doing their usual jobs rather than staged activities.

## Early revenue

Humyn Labs was founded in February by Agarwal, the former CEO of Nazara Technologies, and Ishank Gupta. It says it has 12 paying customers, including frontier AI labs, robotics companies and world-model developers, with about 90% of revenue coming from the US. The founders told TechCrunch it made $10 million in revenue between April and October and has another $20 million in signed contracts. The debt helps cover the gap between paying data collectors weekly and getting paid by customers 30 to 60 days later.

India is becoming a hub for this kind of physical-AI data collection, though recording people in their homes has raised privacy and consent questions.
