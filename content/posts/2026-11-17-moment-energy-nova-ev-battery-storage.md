Moment Energy, a company with operations in Austin and Vancouver, has introduced Nova, a battery energy storage system for commercial and industrial sites that is built from electric vehicle batteries after their life on the road. According to the company's announcement, Nova is the first storage system made from repurposed EV batteries to hold both UL 1973 and UL 9540 safety certifications, and it has also been tested to UL 9540A, the method used to evaluate how thermal runaway fires spread.

## What it is

Each Nova block provides 200 kW of power and 500 kWh of energy in a footprint of about 2.94 m by 1.80 m. Blocks can be used individually at a business or combined into installations in the tens of megawatt-hours. The system can mix batteries from different EV models, and Moment Energy says an AI-driven battery management system tracks and balances packs that are in different states of health.

Packs are swappable, so aging batteries can be replaced without tearing out the enclosure or concrete pad. With its capacity augmentation service, the company says the system can last more than 20 years.

## Why it matters

Most stationary batteries in North America are imported, largely from China. Moment Energy is pitching retired EV packs as a domestic supply, and says Nova is made in North America and has been independently verified as compliant with the U.S. Foreign Entity of Concern rules. The company says that makes Nova eligible for the federal Investment Tax Credit in 2027 and beyond.

Nova is being built at the company's Megafactory 1 and is available to order now, with first deliveries planned for the first quarter of 2027. All figures here come from Moment Energy's announcement.
