The European Space Agency has moved its ClearSpace-1 debris-removal mission into construction. ESA signed a contract at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen on Nov. 17 that makes OHB System the prime contractor for building the spacecraft. OHB says the deal is worth more than €102 million.

## The mission

ClearSpace-1 will go after ESA's own Proba-1, a small technology satellite that launched in 2001 and ended regular operations in 2022. Proba-1 was never built to be grabbed, so the servicer has to work almost entirely on its own. It will use optical and radio-frequency sensors to measure how the target is tumbling and moving, close in, and catch it with a purpose-built capture mechanism. After that, the two spacecraft will be steered into a controlled reentry together.

ESA calls it the first demonstration of capturing and bringing down an "unprepared and uncooperative" satellite. Launch is scheduled for 2030.

## A redesigned project

The mission was originally meant to remove a VESPA payload adapter, but that object was damaged in a debris collision in 2023, so the mission was redesigned around Proba-1. OHB now leads the industrial team. ClearSpace, which did much of the groundwork on the original concept, is still responsible for key rendezvous and capture technology. ESA lists Indra and OHB Sweden as the other subcontractors, while OHB names Deimos Engenharia and OHB Sweden as partners.

ESA says the mission supports its Zero Debris goal for 2030 and is meant to show regulators that a practical way to remove stranded spacecraft now exists.
