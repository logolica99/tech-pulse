Tesla's driverless Cybercab fleet in Texas has passed 1,000 vehicles, at least on paper. EVXL reports that the state's roster of vehicles allowed to run without a driver added 285 Cybercabs on November 26, Thanksgiving Day, the biggest one-day jump yet, bringing the total to 1,107.

## The numbers

- On November 10 Tesla had 573 Cybercabs and 729 Model Ys on the roster. By November 26 it had 1,107 Cybercabs and about 734 Model Ys.
- Tesla Robotaxi LLC now lists 1,841 vehicles in Texas, so Cybercabs make up about 60% of its registered fleet. The newest batch is 2027 model-year cars, according to the FSD Database.
- Waymo's Texas roster showed 1,410 vehicles on November 28. Tesla's lead over Waymo in the registry has grown from five cars to 431.

## What it doesn't say

A registration is permission to drive without a human, not proof that a car is in paid service. Tesla's last official figure was more than 300 Cybercabs running in Austin in October, and it has not published a current active count for Austin or Dallas.

The service is still growing. Paid Cybercab rides began in Austin in September and in Dallas on November 19, and Tesla this week nearly doubled its Dallas service area to about 150 square miles. Tesla's robotaxi mileage counter recently passed 6 million autonomous miles.
