First Light Fusion is bringing in a national lab for the next stage of its fusion concept. UK Research and Innovation said on Nov. 18 that the Science and Technology Facilities Council's Central Laser Facility (CLF), based at the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory, has begun working with the Oxford company on the "fast ignition" physics at the heart of its FLARE approach.

## Two steps to fusion

Fast ignition splits fusion into two steps: first squeeze deuterium-tritium fuel to very high density, then heat it very quickly to start burning. First Light says it has already validated the compression step, testing FLARE's multi-shell compression design on its M3 pulsed-power machine.

The CLF will tackle the heating step. Its scientists will model how a short, intense laser pulse produces energetic electrons, how those electrons travel through the plasma, and how efficiently they dump their energy into the dense fuel. They will look at engineered target structures and magnetic fields to steer electrons into the ignition region, with the goal of cutting the laser energy needed to ignite.

## Designing for a power plant

UKRI says FLARE's cylindrical geometry gives easier access to a large area of compressed fuel than standard laser fast-ignition setups, and that the partners will weigh the physics against cost, efficiency and engineering needs rather than optimizing the laser alone. The aim is to keep fusion gain high while lowering the energy needed to start burn, which would ease demands on the rest of a future plant.

The work is already under way. Over the next year, CLF's modeling is meant to define the laser requirements for ignition and, later, for a possible high-gain demonstrator.
