Swedish autonomous and electric freight company Einride, which trades on Nasdaq as ENRD, says its contracted annual recurring revenue reached about $167 million as of November 2026, up 76% from $95 million at the end of June. The figures come from the company's third-quarter business update.

## The numbers

Einride reported third-quarter revenue of $18.3 million on a constant-currency basis, up 27% from the second quarter. It added eight customers since June, bringing its total to more than 40, and reaffirmed guidance of $39 million to $42 million in revenue for the second half of 2026. It also set a new target of roughly $250 million in annualized run-rate revenue by December 2027, saying more than 65% of that is already covered by contracted revenue.

The business is still burning cash. Adjusted EBITDA was negative $20.3 million for the quarter, and cash fell to about $53 million at the end of September from $77 million three months earlier. The company says it is aiming for cash-flow breakeven in 2028 and uses third-party financing to grow its truck fleet.

## Trucks with no cab

Einride's vehicles are electric and, in its autonomous deployments, have no driver's cab at all. In September it started what it believes is the first cab-less SAE Level 4 truck in daily service on a German public road, hauling goods for Lidl under a permit from Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority. The same month, two cab-less trucks went into daily freight work with EASE Logistics in Marysville, Ohio, and after the quarter it signed a five-year deal to run four autonomous vehicles at a Lorenz Snacks plant in Germany.

The company says it now holds permits to run without a safety driver in four countries and five U.S. states, and that contracted driverless operating hours grew from about 5,400 in June to about 8,500 by mid-November. On the technology side, it is building its next-generation driver on NVIDIA's Hyperion platform and plans to test NVIDIA's Alpamayo vision-language-action model on real roads in the second half of 2027, with highway autonomy targeted from the first half of 2028.
