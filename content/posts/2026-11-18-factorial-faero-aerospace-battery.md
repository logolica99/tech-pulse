Battery maker Factorial Energy has launched FAERO, a new cell platform built for drones and other aerospace uses that need a lot of power without extra weight.

## The problem it targets

Factorial says conventional lithium-ion cells force a trade-off: high-energy cells can't deliver much power, and high-power cells hold far less energy. Drones need both. The company notes that a drone typically draws nearly 3 horsepower per kilowatt-hour continuously, and spikes past 13 during takeoff with a full payload, compared with under half a horsepower per kilowatt-hour for a typical car.

## How FAERO works

FAERO is a direct offshoot of Factorial's FEST lithium-metal cells, which the company says have reached 450 Wh/kg at the cell level. It keeps the same cell design and manufacturing process but swaps in a liquid electrolyte, which Factorial says preserves FEST's energy density while raising power output. It supports the same anode options as FEST, from lithium metal and silicon to anode-free designs.

According to the company, FAERO offers up to 80% higher energy density and up to four times the energy at full power compared with current high-power lithium-ion cells. Those are Factorial's own claims, and its release notes the cells still face development, testing, and qualification.

## Making it

Because FAERO runs on the same equipment as FEST, Factorial says it can shift existing production capacity between the two as demand requires. Aerospace is the first focus, while the company continues its automotive work with partners.
