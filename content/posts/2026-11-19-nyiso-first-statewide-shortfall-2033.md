New York could run short of electricity within the next decade. The New York Independent System Operator (NYISO), which runs the state's grid, said in an assessment released Wednesday that available resources may fall about 225 megawatts short of demand plus required reserves in 2033. That gap is projected to widen to more than 1,850 megawatts by 2036.

NYISO describes it as the first statewide resource deficiency its reliability reviews have identified.

## Why it is happening

Two forces are squeezing the system from both ends: demand is climbing, with data centers a major driver, while the state's aging generation fleet faces a growing risk of failures. The crunch looks even more serious in New York City and the Lower Hudson Valley, according to Bloomberg Law's report on the assessment.

The finding adds New York to the list of grids where the AI and data-center build-out is starting to show up in long-range reliability planning.
