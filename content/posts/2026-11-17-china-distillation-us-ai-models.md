Chinese developers are using output from leading American AI models to train domestic systems in increasingly sophisticated ways, according to a Reuters investigation based on more than 300 Chinese academic papers, patents, corporate disclosures, job postings, and government documents.

## From research trick to industry practice

The technique, called distillation, uses a powerful "teacher" model to generate answers, code, or worked solutions that are then used to train a smaller "student" model. Reuters found that it has spread from academic experiments to mainstream industrial use, with Chinese companies building software to automate the process and specialist workers preparing training material. The documents also show Beijing encouraging distillation to make AI cheaper to deploy across manufacturing.

Distillation is not illegal and is common elsewhere. It cannot fully reproduce a frontier model or remove the need for compute, but it can preserve selected skills in cheaper systems, which helps developers who face limited access to advanced chips.

## Policy friction

The report says the practice could blunt US export controls on AI chips, which Washington moved to tighten further this week. Reuters reports that US Commerce officials raised concerns with Chinese regulators in July that some collection of US model output could amount to IP theft, and that Chinese officials defended distillation as standard practice without making concrete commitments. The issue comes ahead of the November 18-19 APEC summit in Shenzhen, where Presidents Trump and Xi are expected to meet.
