Tesla has started rolling out version 15 of its "Full Self-Driving" driver-assistance software, Electrek reports. The company announced the release on X on November 21 and published release notes saying v15 upgrades every part of its end-to-end driving network.

## What changed

The central change is a new vision encoder that Tesla says is shared between FSD, its Robotaxi service and the Optimus humanoid robot. Tesla says it was trained on millions of internet videos and improves the critical intervention rate by 25 times. That figure comes from Tesla and has not been independently verified.

Other items in Tesla's notes include:

- A new live visualization, called Tesla Augmented Reality, that replaces separate model outputs with a single end-to-end view and uses less compute.
- Navigation driven by a learned model based on fleet route preferences, and parking choices based on a driver's history.
- Better detection of road signs, small animals, debris, potholes and deep standing water.
- Better speed handling in school zones, under speed limits and in bad weather, plus slowing for speed cameras.
- Driver monitoring that works more often with hats and sunglasses.

## Who gets it

Electrek notes that Tesla did not say which vehicles receive v15 first, though the company has previously told investors that its AI4 (HW4) computer can run it. The shared encoder is a sign that Tesla is trying to use one perception stack across cars and robots.
