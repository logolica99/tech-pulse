What if a drone's landing gear was also its battery? Researchers at the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST) have built a flexible lithium-sulfur battery electrode meant to sit inside curved and hollow parts of drones and robots, Interesting Engineering reports.

## Nanowires on carbon cloth

Structural batteries, which carry mechanical loads while storing energy, could save weight, but most designs so far are flat and hard to fit into curved parts. The KAIST team instead grew ultrafine nickel cobalt selenide (NiCo₂Se₄) nanowires directly on woven carbon cloth. The cloth gives a strong, conductive base, and because the nanowires are anchored to it rather than coated on, they are less likely to crack or peel off when bent.

The nanowires also help with two known weaknesses of lithium-sulfur chemistry: sulfur's poor electrical conductivity and the escape of lithium polysulfides, which slowly degrades performance.

## Bent, shaped and broken

The electrode kept its structure through 1,000 bending cycles at a radius of 2.5 millimeters. Built into pouch cells, it powered an LED while installed in oval, square and drone-leg-shaped parts. In one test, the cell kept 99.8% of its starting voltage after the cylindrical frame around it fractured under load.

Professor Il-Kwon Oh, who led the work, said the study shows drone legs and robot frames could serve as energy storage as well as structure. The team cautions that more work is needed to measure how much weight could actually be saved and how much run time added. The results were published in Advanced Composites and Hybrid Materials.
