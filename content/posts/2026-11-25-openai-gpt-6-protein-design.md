OpenAI published research on November 24 claiming its general-purpose GPT-6 models can do protein design work that usually needs specialized biology models.

## What OpenAI found

According to OpenAI, GPT-6 family models can design new protein shapes and the amino acid sequences behind them using only reasoning and general coding tools, without calling dedicated protein-generation models. An internal research model matched or beat specialized models on many design tasks, and researchers could steer designs in plain language, for example by saying where a protein should attach and what shape it should take.

## Lab results

In lab tests run with Onava, proteins based on GPT-6.2's designs showed evidence of binding across five targets, and redesigned antibodies kept their binding. OpenAI notes that specialized models still helped supply sequences and pick which candidates to test, so the lab results are not purely from GPT-6.

## Why it matters

The work is described in a preprint titled "GPT-6 as a Universal Foundation Model for Protein Biology: Beyond Specialized Models." OpenAI's pitch is that one general model that takes natural-language instructions could be easier to adapt than a chain of specialized tools that each need expert handling.
