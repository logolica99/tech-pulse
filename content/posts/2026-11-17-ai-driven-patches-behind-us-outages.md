This week the U.S. has seen a nationwide flight ground stop, bank customers locked out of their accounts, a major Microsoft Outlook outage, and a failure on a classified National Security Agency network. Now a new explanation has surfaced. The Washington Sun reports, citing three people familiar with private briefings, that Wall Street executives and the NSA told members of Congress the disruptions came from attempts to patch security holes found by AI models.

## AI found the bugs, not caused the outages

According to the report, AI did not attack anything. Banks and agencies have been pointing powerful AI models at their own systems to find weaknesses before attackers do. Each discovery creates pressure to patch right away, because adversaries have access to similar tools. The sources said organizations pushed through more fixes at once than their systems could handle, which knocked services over for a while.

"They're trying to push through a lot of patches all at once — more than the system was designed for," one congressional official briefed on the matter told the paper.

## Official explanations so far

In public, the explanations have been vaguer. Banks such as JPMorgan Chase blamed software bugs. The NSA cited "scheduled routine maintenance." Microsoft pointed to a technical defect, and the cause of the FAA failure is still unclear. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told CNBC that new technology is being pushed "on top of really old computer systems" and that more disruptions are likely.

The Washington Sun says the FAA canceled more than 20,000 flights because of the ground stop, citing The Wall Street Journal. Nathan Calvin of the advocacy group Encode told the paper the episode shows how much critical infrastructure depends on vulnerable legacy software. He expects updating it to stay rocky as AI models keep finding more flaws.
