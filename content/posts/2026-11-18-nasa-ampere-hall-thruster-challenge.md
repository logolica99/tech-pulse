NASA has launched the Advanced Mid-Power Electric Rocket (AMPERE) Challenge, a competition for U.S. companies to build prototype mid-power Hall effect thrusters. These electric engines use an electric field to accelerate a propellant gas, giving very efficient thrust for satellites and deep-space robotic spacecraft over long missions.

## How it works

The prize pool is $500,000, with up to $250,000 each for two winning teams. The contest runs in three phases:

- **Phase 1:** teams submit a concept design, manufacturing plan, and cost and performance projections.
- **Phase 2:** up to two selected teams build prototypes alongside NASA staff, with no-cost licensing of NASA-developed Hall thruster technology that runs on krypton.
- **Phase 3:** prototypes are tested at NASA's Glenn Research Center at no cost to the teams.

NASA says the aim is to remove barriers to commercialization by opening up its experts, technology, and facilities. AMPERE is the first entry in a new NASA Tech Maturation Prize series.

## Key dates

A virtual industry day is set for Thursday, Nov. 19, and registration is open until Jan. 27, 2027, at 5 p.m. EST. The underlying project is managed by NASA Glenn and JPL, and the challenge is administered by Yet2.
