Ramon.Space, which builds computers designed to run on satellites, has raised $124 million in a Series B round, the company announced on November 17. Growth equity firm PSG led the round. Memory maker Micron Technology joined as a new strategic investor alongside Senvest and Phoenix Financial, with existing backers StageOne, Deep Insight and Grove Ventures also taking part.

## Why compute in space

Instead of sending raw data back to Earth for processing, Ramon.Space's systems let satellites process, store and move data on board. The company says its hardware has to cope with radiation, extreme temperatures and tight power budgets, and that it already flies on commercial and government missions, including communications mega-constellations and Earth observation satellites.

CEO Avi Shabtai told Globes that cooling is one of the hardest parts, since there is no air in space to carry heat away, so ground data center hardware can't simply be bolted onto a satellite. He named future orbital data centers and satellite-based cellular networks as growth areas, and credited SpaceX with making launches commercially accessible enough to open up the market.

## What the money is for

According to the company, the funding will go to ramping production, speeding up delivery to customers and developing next-generation products, plus expansion into new markets. Globes reports that the company has about 130 employees, plans to roughly double its workforce, and is setting up a new center in Germany in addition to its main operations in Israel and offices in the US and UK.
