Volkswagen Group is bringing vehicle-to-grid charging, where a parked EV can send power back to the grid, to ordinary customers. Its charging subsidiary Elli says it will launch Germany's first mass-market vehicle-to-grid offer in December for owners of Volkswagen, CUPRA and Škoda EVs, with Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles joining through a separate ordering process. More European markets are planned for 2027.

## The hardware

The Elli BiDi Charger is a compact DC unit that charges at up to 11 kW and can also feed power back. It costs €1,995 including VAT and shipping, works indoors or outdoors, supports 400-volt and 800-volt cars, and supports the ISO 15118-20 standard, which Elli says lays the groundwork for other compatible EVs later. The Mobility House Energy is Elli's technology partner, and Elli bundles the charger with a tariff, an app and installation.

## How owners get paid

Customers get a plug-in bonus of €0.24 for every hour the car is connected, whether or not the grid draws any power. Volkswagen says that at 250 hours a month this adds up to as much as €720 in the first contract year, enough to cover electricity for up to 15,500 km of driving. TechRadar reports that sessions must last at least three hours to count and that owners can keep earning €0.40 per kWh when the grid takes power. In the app, owners set when they next need the car and how charged it should be, and Elli manages charging and discharging within those limits.

Elli also says it has finished a vehicle-to-home pilot and is running another field test as its next step.
