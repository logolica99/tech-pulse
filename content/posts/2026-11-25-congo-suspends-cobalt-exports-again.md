The Democratic Republic of Congo, the world's biggest cobalt producer, will stop cobalt exports from November 30 until further notice, its strategic minerals regulator ARECOMS said on November 23, as reported by Reuters.

## What's covered

The suspension applies to all cobalt produced in Congo, from large industrial mines to artisanal operations, and stays until ARECOMS formally lifts it. The regulator blamed a persistent market imbalance: weak demand, rising inventories, excess supply and speculative trading that have pushed prices down since mid-year.

## Second time since 2025

This is the second time since April 2025 that Congo has halted cobalt exports. Prices had recovered briefly from record lows, but exports released under Congo's quota system added supply to a market already dealing with weak battery demand.

## What happens next

ARECOMS will use the pause to rework its quota system and plans to review the measure four months after it starts. It stressed that the review will not automatically restart exports; any resumption needs a separate decision on volumes, quotas and conditions. Cobalt is a key material in many lithium-ion battery chemistries, so battery makers will be watching closely.
