Cognition, the startup behind the Devin coding agent, announced **Devin Bots** on November 17. Instead of handing an agent one task at a time, you give a Bot a larger project or an ongoing goal, such as maintaining an open-source repo, keeping an Android app in step with iOS, or managing releases. The Bot then works out the next steps, hands pieces to several Devin agents running in parallel, reviews what comes back, and decides what to do next.

## How it works

According to Cognition, each Bot is a persistent agent with its own memory and tools, so it keeps context across sessions and over weeks. Fortune reports that a Bot joins a team's Slack as its own member, can be used by several colleagues (including over iMessage), and reaches out to people when it is blocked or needs a decision. The product draws on technology from the Interaction Company, maker of the Poke assistant, which Cognition acquired in July.

CEO Scott Wu told Fortune that Bots are not tied to one AI lab: a Bot picks different models for planning, design, computer use and routine coding, mixing Cognition's own cheaper models with outside ones. Each Bot can also be given its own budget.

## Guardrails

Bots run on separate cloud machines, away from credentials on engineers' laptops, and administrators choose which repositories and tools each one can touch. Their code goes through the same tests as human-written code, and teams decide which changes need human review and which low-risk edits can be merged automatically.

## Early results

Cognition says an internal Bot built Devin's Android app in 44 days, starting from a single instruction on September 28 and reporting progress daily in Slack. The company also told Fortune that Bot usage among dozens of early-access customers, including Decagon, Fireworks AI and Reducto, grew tenfold in two weeks. Fortune frames the launch as Cognition's answer to similar always-on agents from OpenAI (Dots) and Anthropic (Duet), with Devin Bots being among the first built specifically for software development.
