Canada's Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation, Evan Solomon, announced on November 26 in Toronto that the government will add C$700 million to its AI Compute Access Fund. Together with the original C$300 million from the Sovereign AI Compute Strategy, the fund now totals C$1 billion.

## What changes

The fund helps Canadian small and medium-sized companies pay for the computing power they need to build, test and scale AI products. The new phase:

- **Prioritises sovereign compute.** The program guide sets higher cost-sharing for compute that is owned, operated and governed in Canada (up to 80% of eligible costs) than for other Canadian-based providers (up to 50%).
- **Moves to sector calls.** The first call for proposals, open now, targets health and life sciences.
- **Expands eligibility** and updates cost-sharing levels, the government says.

## New recipients

Solomon also announced more than C$52 million for 45 companies under the fund's first phase. That brings total commitments to over C$118 million across 89 Canadian companies working in health care, agriculture, manufacturing, finance and transportation.

The government frames the expansion as part of its "AI for All" national strategy and its Budget 2026 goal of bringing 500 megawatts of sovereign AI compute online, using public money to create demand for domestic cloud and data center providers.
