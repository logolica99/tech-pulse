Neuralink says the US Food and Drug Administration has approved its LINK Pivotal Study, which the company describes as the first pivotal trial of an implantable brain-computer interface (BCI) in the United States.

## What the trial will test

The study will evaluate the safety and effectiveness of Neuralink's BCI system in people with severe paralysis caused by amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). It focuses on how well participants can control digital devices by thought, with an emphasis on outcomes that improve day-to-day independence. It will also keep tracking the implant's long-term safety and reliability across several clinical sites.

## Why it matters

A pivotal trial is the larger stage that typically comes before a company can seek approval to market a medical device. This approval covers the study only, not sale of the implant.

It builds on Neuralink's earlier feasibility studies in the US, Canada and the UK, where the company says more than 30 implanted participants have logged thousands of hours of use. Neuralink says enrollment is now open.
