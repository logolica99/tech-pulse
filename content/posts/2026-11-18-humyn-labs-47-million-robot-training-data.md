Robots can't learn how to fold laundry or work a factory line from internet text. Humyn Labs, a startup based in Bengaluru, India, is betting that recordings of real people doing real work will fill that gap, and it just raised $47 million in Series A funding to scale up.

## The round

The money comes in two parts. According to TechCrunch and the company's own announcement, $26 million is equity led by Accel, with Prosus Ventures, BeeGlobal, Bajaj-backed funds, Dallas Venture Capital, Lighthouse Canton, and Jump Trading also participating. The other $21 million is venture debt led by Lighthouse Canton, with Trifecta Capital joining.

Co-founder Manish Agarwal told TechCrunch the debt is there to cover working capital: Humyn Labs pays its data collectors daily or weekly, but its AI lab customers typically pay 30 to 60 days later.

## What it does

The company built its own two-camera capture hardware to film people doing everyday tasks, then uses software to track hand movements and object interactions and label the footage so models can learn from it. It collects data in more than 20 countries across Latin America and Asia, with Brazil, India, and South Korea as its main hubs. Agarwal said it records people doing their normal jobs and chores rather than staged activities.

Humyn Labs was founded in February by Agarwal, formerly CEO of Nazara Technologies, and Ishank Gupta, formerly of AB InBev. It told TechCrunch it has 12 paying customers, including frontier AI labs, robotics companies, and world-model developers, with about 90% of revenue from the U.S. Agarwal said it made $10 million in revenue from April to October and has another $20 million in signed contracts. The company's announcement separately claims more than $50 million in annualized revenue.

## Why it matters

Real-world data is one of the main bottlenecks for physical AI, and a growing set of startups, including several in India, are racing to supply it. TechCrunch notes that recording people inside their homes also raises privacy and consent questions for this kind of business. Humyn Labs says it will use the new money to expand in Brazil, South Korea, and India, scale its hardware and labeling pipeline, and grow teams in San Francisco, London, and Seoul.
