The COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, ended on Sunday, November 22, without an agreed final pact. After two weeks of talks and two extra days of overtime, the longest UN climate conference on record, the Turkish COP president Murat Kurum told the closing plenary there was no agreement on the text, so its decisions were not adopted. CNBC notes the last time UN climate talks failed to adopt a deal was in Copenhagen in 2009.

## Why it fell apart

The main fight was over fossil fuels. The European Union formally objected because the draft made no reference to fossil fuels or a transition away from them, which Ireland's environment minister called "unconscionable." Panama and the Marshall Islands also refused to back it. CNBC reports that Saudi Arabia and other oil producers had lobbied to keep fossil fuels out of the text. Australia's Chris Bowen, who led negotiations as co-president, said fuels and climate finance were the deal-breakers.

## What was in the failed draft

According to CNBC, the draft acknowledged that warming is on track to overshoot 1.5°C and called for faster development of carbon-removal technology, which critics feared would be used to justify continued oil production. It also included a Turkish goal to raise electricity's share of final world energy use to 35% by 2035, from about 23% now, and a call to triple adaptation finance without a stated baseline.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres called the outcome "deeply disappointing" but said "there is no ambiguity in the science." UN climate chief Simon Stiell pointed to an agreement on a just transition and Action Agenda initiatives on electrification and investment. The failure does not undo commitments from earlier COPs, and the unresolved issues move to COP32 in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.
