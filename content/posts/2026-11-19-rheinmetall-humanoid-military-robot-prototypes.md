Germany's biggest defense company is joining the humanoid robot race. Rheinmetall CEO Armin Papperger said the company is putting a lot of money into humanoid robots for the military and expects first prototypes in roughly 15 to 18 months. He said those prototypes would be hardened for military use.

Papperger made the remarks in an interview with RTL/ntv at the Süddeutsche Zeitung economic summit. German defense site hartpunkt reported them on November 16, and Defence24 and HumanoidMegaHub picked the story up on November 19.

## Upper body first, AI is the hard part

Engineers are currently working on the robot's upper body, Papperger said. He argued that many companies can already build robots that talk or help in care homes, but a machine that may have to fight is a different matter. It needs enough AI to judge situations, communicate with soldiers and work alongside them. "That is not so easy," he said, in remarks translated from German.

He did not say what military tasks the robots would do, whether they would carry weapons, or when production might start. hartpunkt notes that showing prototypes around 2028 suggests real operational use is unlikely before the 2030s.

## Not Rheinmetall's first robot

Rheinmetall already builds the Mission Master family of uncrewed ground vehicles, which handle transport, reconnaissance, medical evacuation and fire support. They use the company's PATH autonomy system to navigate, avoid obstacles and follow soldiers, Defence24 reports. A walking robot that handles objects in messy terrain is a much bigger software challenge.

HumanoidMegaHub points out the contrast with Figure AI, which says it will stay out of defense work.
