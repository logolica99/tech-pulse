General Flash, a Cambridge, Massachusetts startup spun out of MIT, came out of stealth this week with $128 million in combined seed and Series A funding. Investors include Lux Capital, Construct Capital, Overmatch Ventures, Lowercarbon Capital, GreatPoint Ventures and others.

## A different way to make metal

The company's process, which it calls solid-state flash electrolysis, heats metal oxide powders and hits them with strong electric fields. Founder and CEO Ric Fulop told Heatmap this pushes the material into a state between solid and liquid where oxygen escapes much faster, leaving pure metal behind. General Flash says the process has no direct emissions and works across many metals, including tungsten, titanium, tantalum, zirconium, hafnium and rare earth elements.

Heatmap notes it could be an alternative to molten salt electrolysis, the method China mainly uses for some of these metals, though General Flash covers only the refining step. Mining and oxide processing for materials like rare earths and tungsten still run largely through China.

## Why now

These metals go into semiconductors, defense, aerospace and advanced manufacturing, and the company says China holds more than 80% of refining capacity for many of them. It also points to US defense rules that, starting January 1, 2027, will bar Chinese supply of many of these materials from defense supply chains.

General Flash says it has proven the process at a multi-ton pilot facility near MIT. It has broken ground on a 1,000-metric-ton-per-year plant in Massachusetts and says the new funding fully pays for that first plant, with production targeted for summer 2027.
