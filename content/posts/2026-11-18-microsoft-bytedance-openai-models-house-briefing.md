The House Select Committee on China asked Microsoft on November 18 to brief lawmakers on reported talks to sell ByteDance more AI cloud services, including access to OpenAI's models.

## The reported deal

The Information reported that Microsoft is nearing a deal worth about $500 million that would let ByteDance, the Chinese technology company, use OpenAI models through Microsoft's Azure cloud. Reuters, citing that report, says Microsoft is also trying to sell OpenAI models to other Chinese companies, including Alibaba. OpenAI blocks most Chinese firms from using its AI. Microsoft has the right to resell OpenAI's technology and, according to the report, doesn't apply the same limits.

## Washington's reaction

Committee chairman John Moolenaar, a Michigan Republican, said the panel wants details "given serious concerns over American companies helping China catch up to the United States in the AI race." He said "Microsoft should not sell AI tools to ByteDance." He also argued that Microsoft, as a Pentagon contractor, should not be "playing both sides" of the US-China AI competition.

A Microsoft spokesperson said the company "takes misuse of its AI services seriously" and is committed to enforcing its terms of service, following the law, and honoring its partner agreements. The spokesperson added that Microsoft does not comment on confidential customer negotiations.

The request comes as Washington is already tightening Chinese access to advanced US computing, including through overseas cloud services. That broader backdrop is why the reported deal drew attention so fast.
