Chinese charging and storage company StarCharge announced on November 27 that a 1 GW/4 GWh battery plant it built in Xilin Gol League, Inner Mongolia, is connected to the grid. The company calls it the world's largest single-site grid-forming standalone battery system.

## The plant

- **Size:** 1 GW of power and 4 GWh of energy, so it can discharge at full power for about four hours.
- **Hardware:** 800 of StarCharge's own 5 MWh battery cabinets using lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells, on a 28-hectare site.
- **Cost:** about RMB 3 billion (roughly US$420 million), according to the company.
- **Track record:** StarCharge says it has already run stably for three months since connection.

## Why grid-forming matters

Most grid batteries are grid-following: they sync to the voltage and frequency the grid already sets. A grid-forming system sets voltage and frequency itself, which lets it actively steady the grid, respond quickly to faults and even run in island mode. That makes it more useful in regions with a lot of wind and solar and fewer large spinning generators.

StarCharge says the station is designed to deliver about 1 TWh of green electricity a year to Sonid Left Banner and nearby areas, and that it built the project with central state-owned partners. It is one of the company's flagship storage projects and a sign of how large standalone Chinese batteries are getting.
