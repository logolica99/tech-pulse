A study published November 16, 2026 in Nature Sustainability gives one of the first facility-by-facility estimates of how much power America's largest data centers use and where that power comes from.

## The numbers

Researchers led by Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health and the Harvard Data Science Initiative used satellite imagery and industry data to list 403 hyperscale data centers operating between May 2024 and April 2025. They estimated each site's electricity use under several load scenarios, then used EPA power-plant data to attribute emissions based on the mix of generation in each facility's regional grid.

- Across scenarios, the facilities used roughly 68 to 99 terawatt-hours; the central estimate is about 82 TWh, around 1.8% of total US electricity consumption.
- Attributable emissions were about 25.7 million metric tons of CO2 in the central case (21 to 31 million across scenarios). Harvard says that is two to three times an earlier estimate for 2018.
- About 54% of the attributed electricity came from fossil fuels, mostly natural gas (38%) and coal (15%).
- Virginia, Oregon, Ohio, and Iowa together accounted for more than half of the electricity studied.

The Verge, citing MIT's John Sterman, notes that 82 TWh is more than about 148 individual countries use each year.

## Caveats

The authors used modeled loads rather than metered data, and regional grid averages rather than tracing the power delivered to each site. The study covers only the 403 validated facilities, not every US data center, and cannot separate AI workloads from general cloud computing. The researchers also say demand has likely grown substantially since the study period ended in April 2025.
