Rockwell Automation, the Milwaukee industrial automation giant, said on November 18 that it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire AGILOX, an Austrian company that builds autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) for factories and warehouses. Rockwell did not disclose a price.

## What Rockwell is buying

AGILOX, founded in 2017 and based in Neukirchen bei Lambach, makes forklift-style AMRs that it says run on in-house AI and swarm networking. According to Rockwell's announcement, the company has deployed more than 2,500 robots, mainly in automotive but also in third-party logistics, food and beverage, and pharmaceuticals, and employs over 250 people across Europe, the US, and China.

## Why it matters

Rockwell already sells flatbed AMRs under its OTTO brand, which is strongest in North America. AGILOX's base is mostly in Europe. Rockwell says putting the two lines together gives it a full autonomous material-handling lineup and will more than double its AMR business. The company frames the deal as part of its "physical AI" strategy: moving customers from fixed automation to robots that move parts around the whole plant floor on their own.

The deal still needs regulatory approval and other closing conditions. Rockwell expects it to close in the second quarter of its fiscal 2027, after which AGILOX will sit in Rockwell's Intelligent Devices segment.
