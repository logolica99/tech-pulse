Nvidia is in advanced discussions to acquire Inception, a Bay Area startup that builds diffusion-based large language models, according to a report from The Information that cites people with knowledge of the talks. The proposed deal is worth at least $500 million, plus earnout awards for Inception staff that pay out if they hit certain goals. Neither company has announced a deal.

## What Inception builds

Inception is about two years old and has more than 30 employees. It drew attention in early 2025 with a "diffusion" LLM. Models like ChatGPT and Claude are autoregressive: they write a reply one token at a time, left to right. A diffusion model instead starts with a rough, noisy draft of the whole answer and refines it over several passes. The main appeal is speed, especially for long responses.

The startup had raised about $50 million from investors including Menlo Ventures, M12, Mayfield and Nvidia itself. Its co-founders, machine learning professors Stefano Ermon (Stanford), Aditya Grover (UCLA) and Volodymyr Kuleshov (Cornell), are expected to join Nvidia full time, according to the report.

## Why Nvidia wants it

According to the report, the technology and team would go toward Nemotron, Nvidia's family of open models aimed at companies and governments that do not want to depend on closed models from Anthropic or OpenAI. Nvidia released a Nemotron version in May that mixes diffusion with autoregressive generation, and researchers involved also plan to apply the work to "physical" models that help robots perceive their surroundings.

The talks reportedly began before Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO, and Microsoft had earlier shown interest in a similar deal. The report frames the talks as a sign that Nvidia's AI acquisitions are continuing despite its leadership turmoil.
