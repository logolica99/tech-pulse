The US government now has an official shortlist of American AI bundles it wants to sell abroad. The Commerce Department's International Trade Administration said it has designated an initial 33 "pre-set consortia" under what it now calls the American SI (Super Intelligence) Exports Program, which carries out President Trump's executive order on exporting the American AI technology stack.

## What a package includes

Each designated consortium had to cover every layer of the stack Commerce defines: AI-optimized hardware, data pipelines and labeling, models, security and cybersecurity, and applications. The packages go on a "menu" that US Commercial Service officers and other agencies will offer to foreign governments and customers.

## What designation gets you

According to the release, designated packages get priority government-to-government engagement, priority export-control review, and priority routing for federal financing referrals. Commerce did not say how much faster licences will move or how much financing is available. Times of AI notes that the program's earlier notice said designation does not guarantee a licence, financing or a sale.

A second, on-demand phase opens for applications on November 30, 2026. It lets companies get the same support for a specific overseas deal while covering only the stack layers that deal needs, rather than offering a full stack.

## The "national champions" label

The Wall Street Journal had reported that the administration would name more than two dozen companies, including Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, OpenAI and Anthropic, as AI "national champions," according to Times of AI's summary of that report. Commerce's own release does not use that phrase and does not name companies in its text; it links to a separate list of the consortia.

The designations landed alongside a new Commerce rule on AI chips in overseas data centers, pairing tighter limits for China-linked buyers with promotion of approved American bundles.
