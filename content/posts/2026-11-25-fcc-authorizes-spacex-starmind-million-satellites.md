The US Federal Communications Commission on Wednesday, November 25, granted SpaceX conditional authorization for a constellation of up to 1 million Starmind satellites designed to act as data centers in orbit. If fully built, it would multiply the number of satellites in orbit roughly 50 times.

## A long runway

The order gives SpaceX seven years to put up its first operational Starmind satellite, until November 2035 to reach 100,000, and until November 2040 for the full constellation. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr framed the decision as opening a new frontier for the computing infrastructure behind US growth.

## Strings attached

SpaceX proposed six orbital shells between about 550 km and 1,000 km and asked for a waiver to retire satellites above 600 km into other Earth orbits or around the Sun instead of burning them up in the atmosphere. The FCC declined that waiver for now, saying SpaceX had not given enough information, so any satellite not meant to reenter needs separate approval before launch.

## Still unproven

Ars Technica notes that the approval clears a regulatory hurdle but does not show orbital data centers are technically or economically viable, and that the FCC dismissed many environmental concerns raised in public comments.
