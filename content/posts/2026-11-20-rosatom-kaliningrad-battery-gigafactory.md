Russia's state nuclear company Rosatom has started up its first large battery factory. Its fuel division, managed by TVEL, has put a lithium-ion "gigafactory" in the Neman district of the Kaliningrad region into pilot operation, according to a company release published by AK&M.

## How big it is

The plant is rated to produce batteries totaling 4 gigawatt-hours of capacity a year. Rosatom says it is the only large-scale, full-cycle lithium-ion battery production in Russia, covering everything from the cell's base chemistry to finished modules and complete battery packs.

## Why Rosatom is doing it

Rosatom CEO Alexey Likhachev framed the launch as a step toward "technological sovereignty." Energy storage is part of Rosatom's push into non-nuclear businesses, and he said it ties into a wider lithium chain, from mining raw materials through recycling spent batteries, as well as the company's electric mobility work. He said the plant was built and commissioned on schedule with support from the Kaliningrad regional government and Russia's industry ministry.

The details come from Rosatom's own announcement; AK&M notes the release is based on company materials.
