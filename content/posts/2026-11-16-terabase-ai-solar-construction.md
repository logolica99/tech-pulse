Terabase Energy released the next generation of Construct on November 16, 2026. The Berkeley company is calling the product an AI construction execution system for utility-scale solar, storage, and wind.

Terabase says Construct already sits on more than 35 gigawatts of projects. This release adds an agent inside the product that answers questions in plain language, builds reports, and flags deviations from the plan. The agent draws on daily drone imagery, data entered by crews, and live locations from equipment beacons. A small magnetic beacon, the company says, can be attached to machinery and even portable toilets, so a team can see idle time and utilization across a site that may cover 1,500 to 2,500 acres for a single 300 megawatt plant.

## What the software compares

A second piece, which Terabase calls Energy Intelligence, starts when the first medium-voltage circuit is energized. Construct uses the company's PlantPredict model to estimate what the as-built plant should produce and compares that figure with measured output, down to the tracker. The same model is meant to set the baseline for the capacity test, so a shortfall is assigned to a cause instead of an "unknown losses" bucket.

Pierre Gousseland, senior vice president of product and a co-founder, framed the release around timing. A defect fixed while a crew is still on the row takes minutes. The same defect found at commissioning means another crew, a truck, and lost days.

The announcement quotes two customers. Joshua Hauger, director of construction at MasTec, said the software gives field teams and leadership a shared view of performance. Amit Nadkarni, chief operating officer at Doral Renewables, said a single portfolio view lets his team spot issues earlier as the pipeline grows.

Terabase also says an autonomous drone-in-a-box fleet flies more than 3 gigawatts of active construction every day, and that a private mesh network keeps that data moving where cell service is thin. The company planned to show the release at RE+ 2026 in Las Vegas, November 17–19, at booth C6682.

Those figures are Terabase's, from its own announcement. The release does not include an independent count of hours saved or defects caught.
