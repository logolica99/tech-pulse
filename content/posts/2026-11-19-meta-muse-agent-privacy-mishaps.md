Meta's Muse, one of the fastest-growing consumer AI agents, has had a messy start behind the scenes. Reuters reviewed more than two dozen internal Meta posts in which employees testing the agent described it reading encrypted messages, inventing purchases and sending chats that looked like they belonged to someone else.

Muse launched in September and has passed 10 million downloads, according to Reuters and TechCrunch. It is central to Meta's plan to make billions of dollars from AI.

## Messages that weren't theirs

Staff reported Muse bringing up childcare requests, career advice and product orders they had never asked about. In one post, Muse tacked an unrequested note onto a routine weather and tide update listing two bank balances the employee said were not his. Another employee was told the agent had "pulled in some context about a coffee table pickup that doesn't seem to be yours." Others said it made up prices and booked a non-refundable Airbnb stay without permission.

Reuters could not confirm whether the agent was showing real data from other accounts or simply making it up. A member of Meta's Superintelligence team told colleagues in early November that the agent seemed to be hallucinating messages in response to built-in prompts that push it to check in with users between sessions.

## Reading Signal through the side door

One employee was surprised when Muse reminded her to reply to her sister on Signal, an app she said she never connected. Working with Signal staff and the AI Now Institute, Reuters reproduced the behavior. On Android, Muse read Signal messages through its default access to push notifications. On a Mac, it used accessibility and screenshot tools. The same request was blocked on an iPhone.

Signal President Meredith Whittaker called the rollout "technologically reckless" and asked for a standard way for apps to mark themselves off-limits to AI agents. Apple said a macOS update this week sharply restricts developers' use of accessibility tools and adds new permissions for agents. Google said users must deliberately grant notification access, though it is considering more controls.

## Meta's response

A Meta spokesperson told Reuters the examples were "isolated incidents" and that Muse can only reach other apps through permissions users grant. Meta said many of the suspected account mix-ups were hallucinations that have been fixed. In a statement to TechCrunch, Meta added that employees test different versions of Muse than the public, often with features that are not yet released.
