South Korean shipbuilder Samsung Heavy Industries and US reactor startup Tesser Atomics have agreed to work together, exclusively, on a demonstration project for the first nuclear-powered liquefied natural gas (LNG) carrier. The two signed a memorandum of understanding in Norway on November 19.

## The deal

Under the agreement, Samsung Heavy Industries is Tesser's only shipbuilding partner for the project, and Tesser is the shipyard's only reactor partner. Samsung brings ship design, integration and construction; Tesser brings the reactor and the licensing work. The partners plan to develop the ship concept and the marine version of the reactor first, with the stated path running from concept validation to a demonstration ship and then repeat builds. The demonstration vessel is expected to carry LNG commercially between the United States and Europe. No shipbuilding contract or delivery date was announced.

## The reactor

Tesser's Triton is a compact fluoride-salt-cooled high-temperature reactor designed for ships. It uses TRISO particle fuel and a low-pressure molten salt coolant, and it can be refueled while running. Tesser, based in Texas, was co-founded by CEO Per Peterson, who also co-founded Kairos Power. Tesser is taking the same family of salt-cooled reactor technology to shipping while Kairos stays focused on grid power.

The project grew out of NuProShip, a Norwegian research program that compared reactor options for merchant ships, and continues under SFI SAINT, a consortium led by the Norwegian University of Science and Technology and supported by the Research Council of Norway.

## Context

World Nuclear News notes that Samsung Heavy Industries already received Approval in Principle in September 2025 for a 174,000-cubic-metre LNG carrier concept powered by a molten salt reactor designed with the Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute. Shipping burns about 350 million tonnes of fossil fuel a year and produces roughly 3% of global carbon emissions, according to the same report.
