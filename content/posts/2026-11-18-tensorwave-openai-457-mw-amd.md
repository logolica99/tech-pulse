OpenAI has lined up another big block of computing power, and this one runs entirely on AMD. TensorWave, a Las Vegas cloud company that only uses AMD hardware, said it will provide OpenAI with 457 megawatts of capacity under a six-year agreement announced on Nov. 16.

## What's in the deal

The capacity will be built on AMD's Instinct MI455X GPUs, packed into AMD's Helios rack-scale systems and linked with Ethernet. It will be spread across several US data center sites and reserved for OpenAI alone, with no other tenants. TensorWave expects the first capacity to come online in the first half of 2027 and the full buildout to be done by early 2028.

Neither company disclosed what the contract is worth or where the sites are.

## Why it matters

The deal sits under OpenAI's existing partnership with AMD, and TensorWave is the first GPU-focused "neocloud" the two have publicly named as a supplier under it. It is also TensorWave's largest deployment so far. The company, which was the first public cloud to run AMD's MI300X, raised a $350 million Series B in June and says it has secured more than 2 gigawatts of long-term data center capacity.

OpenAI's compute chief, Uday Ruddarraju, said the agreement advances the company's AMD partnership, while TensorWave co-founder Piotr Tomasik framed it as a sign that delivering hundreds of megawatts quickly is now what separates serious AI infrastructure providers from the rest.
