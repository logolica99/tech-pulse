Volvo Cars has brought AEON humanoid robots from Hexagon's robotics division into its plant in Ghent, Belgium. It's the first Volvo factory to try humanoids, and the robots are learning next to workers and the existing automation.

## How it works

Volvo's own team is training the robots together with Hexagon, using imitation learning, where the robot learns a task by copying how it's done. The first job is placing components into two fixtures with high precision. Once that works, the team plans harder tasks: handling several parts, moving around the production area and working with the plant's automated systems.

According to Volvo, AEON is 1.65 m tall, weighs 60 kg and carries up to 10 kg. It runs on wheels rather than feet, at up to 2.4 m/s, and uses cameras and sensors to see what's around it.

## Timeline

- **Now:** training and validation on real industrial tasks.
- **First half of 2027:** a factory pilot, with more AEON units arriving in Ghent.
- **From mid-2027:** the goal is to use them in real production.

Volvo says the point is to hand the robots some of the most physically demanding, repetitive work. Its argument for the human shape is that a humanoid can fit into the line as it already exists, while fixed robot arms suit stable jobs in one place.
