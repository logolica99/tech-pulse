Two of China's leading AI labs are feeling the squeeze from Washington's newest compute rule and from US labs' efforts to stop distillation, Reuters reports, citing people familiar with the matter.

## Z.AI's flagship is late

Z.AI (Zhipu AI) had planned to ship its next flagship, GLM-5.5, this month. The model is designed at roughly 3 trillion parameters and still hasn't come out. One person said tougher safeguards against distillation (training a model on the answers of a stronger one) are part of the holdup, and that the new US compute restrictions could push the launch into the first quarter of 2027 or later. On Monday Z.AI did release GLM-5.4, built on an existing 750-billion-parameter model.

## Moonshot's cycle could double

Moonshot AI rents much of its training compute and prefers overseas providers, which leaves it especially exposed to the Commerce Department rule that took effect November 19. Internally it expects its usual six-month gap between major releases could stretch to a year or more if overseas access is cut off entirely, and Kimi K4 could slip to around mid-2027, according to Reuters' source. Moonshot is reportedly trying to rent compute in Europe and plans to approach Chinese chipmakers about training on their processors.

Reuters also reports that as it got harder to pull training data from OpenAI and Anthropic models, Moonshot began distilling from Google and Meta models. Moonshot has previously said Kimi K3's gains came from its own architecture changes.

## Background

Anthropic said in September it had seen nearly 200 million exchanges with Claude tied to suspected distillation campaigns, which it attributed to Chinese developers including Alibaba, Moonshot and Z.AI.
