Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia's CEO last week, has agreed to answer lawmakers' questions. According to a press release from the House Select Committee on the CCP, Huang will appear on December 1 for a transcribed interview with that committee, the House Foreign Affairs Committee, and the House Intelligence Committee. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter agreed to a transcribed interview with the same panels on December 2.

The three chairmen, John Moolenaar, Brian Mast, and Rick Crawford, said in a joint statement that they want answers on the roles Huang and Nvidia may have played in letting dangerous Chinese actors get access to advanced chips, and on "the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal."

## Under threat of subpoena

CBS News reports that the House interview will be closed-door, and that Huang will also testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on a date not yet set, per a spokesperson for chairman Tim Scott. The House chairmen had warned in a November 12 letter that refusing to cooperate would lead the Foreign Affairs Committee to issue a subpoena, and negotiations ran past their November 13 deadline.

The inquiry centers on Huang's relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed. CBS notes Megaspeed is under investigation in the US and Singapore over allegedly smuggling export-restricted Nvidia chips into China, which the company denies. A representative for Huang declined to comment to CBS.
