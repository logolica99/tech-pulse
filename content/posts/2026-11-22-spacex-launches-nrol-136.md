SpaceX launched the NROL-136 mission for the US National Reconnaissance Office (NRO) on Sunday, November 22, at 4:23 a.m. PT from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. The NRO says the payload reached orbit, and SpaceX reports the first stage landed at Landing Zone 4 at Vandenberg about seven and a half minutes after liftoff.

## Part of a bigger constellation

The NRO says the mission is its fourth "proliferated" launch this year, part of an effort to build what it calls the largest satellite constellation in US government history. Space.com reports that NROL-136 is the 15th launch dedicated to that proliferated architecture, which uses satellites built with SpaceX and Northrop Grumman and is meant to deliver more frequent surveillance coverage. The first of those launches was NROL-146 in May 2024. As with all classified missions, the NRO has not said how many satellites were on board or what they carry.

## A well-used booster

SpaceX says this was the 14th flight of the booster, which previously launched Sentinel-6B, Twilight, NROL-172, Transporter-17, USSF-259 and eight Starlink missions.
