Researchers and engineers at several Chinese humanoid robot makers, including EV maker Xpeng and robot company Engine AI, have been denied US visas, Nikkei Asia reported on November 19 (Asia time). The refusals threaten their plans to show off hardware at the Consumer Electronics Show in Las Vegas in January.

## How widespread it is

According to the Korea Economic Daily's summary of the Nikkei report, the rejections hit technical staff applying for B-1/B-2 business and tourist visas, while most marketing employees were approved. One Chinese robot company saw all 15 of its technical staff refused and dropped out of CES, and another had about half of its applicants rejected. Chris Pereira, founder of Impact, a consultancy that helps Chinese companies expand abroad, estimated that roughly 40% of his clients preparing for CES had been denied, mostly executives and technical staff in AI and robotics.

This is not the first time. Dozens of engineers from Chinese humanoid companies reportedly missed CES in January 2026 for the same reason, forcing some demos to be cancelled. AgiBot and Unitree are still on the exhibitor list for the coming show and plan to send staff from mainland China.

## Part of a wider squeeze

The denials come as Washington tightens rules on Chinese robots more broadly. The report notes that the FCC introduced restrictions on new foreign-made robots in July, and that President Trump said last month that imported robots, machinery and parts could threaten national security, raising the prospect of tariffs or quotas. At the same time, US humanoid makers still lean heavily on Chinese suppliers for key parts such as actuators and reducers.
