A bipartisan coalition of 42 state attorneys general is trying to stop Character.AI from selling its users' chat logs as part of its Chapter 11 bankruptcy, The Verge reports.

## What the states object to

The AGs say the chatbot company plans to auction "the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts — more than 2.1 million of them registered to minors — with no safeguards for the people whose conversations are being sold."

Their main complaints:

- No requirement that a buyer honor Character.AI's existing privacy policies.
- No way for users to get their data deleted.
- No limits on what a buyer could do with the data, including reselling it.

The AGs also flagged that the first bidder was only set up on November 13, 2026, and lists no parent company or investors.

## Background

Character.AI filed for bankruptcy in October after years of scandals and lawsuits. Disney bought some of its assets and hired CEO Karandeep Anand and part of the technical team, a deal that also drew opposition from a broad group of AGs. Several states, including New York, Connecticut, California and Colorado, have chatbot laws taking effect on January 1, 2027 that the company may run afoul of.
