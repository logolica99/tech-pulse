Citibank has changed its Online User Agreement to make clear that customers who let third-party AI tools into their bank accounts carry the risk for whatever those tools do, TechCrunch reports.

## What changed

AI agents can be handed bank credentials so they can analyze spending, give advice, or even pay bills and move money. Citi's new terms say customers who grant that access are responsible for activity carried out through the agents. TechCrunch says the update also covers the worst case: if the AI tool leaks the credentials or is hacked, the customer is responsible for what attackers do with the account.

A Citi spokesperson told TechCrunch the bank wants customers to understand the risks of sharing credentials with these tools. The terms exempt AI agents that Citi itself provides.

## A wider bank trend

Citi is not alone. According to TechCrunch:

- JPMorgan and Bank of America announced similar policies last month.
- PNC changed its terms on November 18.
- Citizens emailed customers this week warning that they are liable for their AI assistants' actions.

TechCrunch notes that some AI companies are steering clear of the problem. Anthropic's recently launched personal finance tool is described as read-only and cannot move or invest money. The report also points out that banks see agents as a competitive threat. Tools that help people avoid fees or low-interest accounts could eat into bank margins, so banks have a reason to control how agents reach their customers.
