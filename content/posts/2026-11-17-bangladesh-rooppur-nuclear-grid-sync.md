Bangladesh produced grid electricity from nuclear power for the first time on Tuesday, 17 November. According to The Energy Tribune, Unit 1 of the Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant, on the Padma River in Pabna's Ishwardi upazila, was synchronised with the national grid that afternoon and fed in 350 megawatts. Science and Technology Minister Faqir Mahbub Anam was present.

## What happens next

The first grid run was planned to last eight to ten hours so engineers could watch the turbine, generator and grid connection under real load. Bangladesh Atomic Energy Commission officials said the unit would then go offline for about 28 hours of checks, followed by a second test window of four to six hours.

After that comes a commissioning and trial period of about 10 months. The reactor is a Russian VVER-1200 design. It will only be raised gradually toward its full 1,200 MW once it meets the safety rules of both Rosatom, the Russian contractor, and the Bangladesh Atomic Energy Regulatory Authority. Full commercial generation is expected in about a year. Power delivered during testing is not billed.

## Why it matters

Rooppur has two VVER-1200 units for a combined 2,400 MW. That is a big addition to a power system that has relied heavily on imported gas, coal and furnace oil. To handle a single 1,200 MW unit, the Power Grid Company of Bangladesh upgraded high-voltage lines and built large river-crossing towers.

The project is also the largest infrastructure investment in Bangladesh's history. The estimated cost is Tk 138,686 crore, and about 90% of it is financed by Russian state loans. Costs have risen by about Tk 26,000 crore, mostly because the taka has weakened against the dollar, and full completion has slipped to June 2028. The consumer tariff for Rooppur's electricity has not been set yet.
