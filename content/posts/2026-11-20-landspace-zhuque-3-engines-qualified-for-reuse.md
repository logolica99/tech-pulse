LandSpace says the nine engines that flew on its Zhuque-3 Y2 first stage in August are ready to fly again. All nine TQ-12A liquid oxygen and methane engines passed ground acceptance hot-fire tests, moving China closer to reflying a recovered orbital-class booster.

## From landing pad to test stand

The booster landed on deployable legs in Gansu province in August, China's first land recovery of an orbital-class first stage. LandSpace then removed the engines one by one and shipped them to its propulsion base in Huzhou, Zhejiang, for inspection and refurbishment. Some key parts were checked and some fasteners were replaced or tightened.

Each engine then fired for about 185 seconds at different thrust levels and propellant mixture ratios. According to the company, the results matched the pre-flight baselines and met delivery standards. It is the first time LandSpace has overhauled and re-fired engines that actually flew and survived heat, vibration and landing loads.

## Why it matters

LandSpace says the first stage makes up about 70% of the rocket's build cost, while propellant is only 1 to 3% of launch expenses, so reusing the engine cluster is key to cutting prices. The company aims to bring Zhuque-3 launch costs below 20,000 yuan (about $3,000) per kilogram. It plans to keep trimming the teardown and checks between flights as it gathers more data, to shorten the time from recovery to reflight.
