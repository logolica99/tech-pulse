Nvidia has stopped selling its H200 AI accelerators in China. Acting CEO Colette Kress told analysts on the company's November 17 earnings call that Nvidia is "no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China," according to Gizmodo. The H200 is the Hopper-generation chip the U.S. had allowed Nvidia to sell to approved Chinese customers.

## The end of a Huang-era deal

The H200 arrangement was worked out between former CEO Jensen Huang and President Donald Trump. TechCrunch reports that the chips had to pass through the U.S. so the government could take a 25% cut. China hawks hated the deal. Huang argued that walking away from the Chinese market would hurt American tech companies. Gizmodo, citing Bloomberg, says Nvidia sold $2.4 billion worth of H200s to China in the third quarter alone.

The decision came on the same day Nvidia's board created a committee to oversee national security, export control compliance and trade risk. It is the company's first earnings report since Huang resigned last week. On the call, board chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate him.

## Tighter rules elsewhere

The move also follows new U.S. rules, issued Monday, that bar advanced American chips from foreign data centers that serve Chinese customers remotely. TechCrunch notes that Jefferies estimated those rules could cut $44 billion to $62 billion from Nvidia's data center revenue next year. Kress said the backlog of demand for chips such as Vera Rubin means the rules will not affect top-line revenue.

China had already been refusing many H200 deliveries in favor of domestic chips, according to a Georgetown CSET researcher quoted by TechCrunch. The bigger long-term risk for Nvidia may be losing data center customers in Southeast Asia that cannot certify they have no Chinese users.
