San Francisco-based Linq has raised $90 million to build infrastructure that lets AI agents talk to people and use software. Andreessen Horowitz led the round, and Ben Horowitz is joining Linq's board. Law firm Gunderson Dettmer, which represented the company, describes the round as a Series B. TQ Ventures, Mucker Capital and Abstract invested again, and Anthos and Left Lane Capital are new backers.

## From messaging to calls and computers

Linq began with messaging APIs that connect a company's AI agent to customers over chat channels such as Apple Messages for Business, RCS and WhatsApp. The company says it has processed more than 1 billion messages for customers including DoorDash, which uses Linq so people can choose food, build a cart and check out in a single text thread. Linq is also an Apple Messaging Service Provider.

The company is now going beyond text. A computer use product gives agents access to physical computing infrastructure so they can operate software and finish tasks. A product called HD Calling for Agents adds voice calls. Linq says the new money will go toward messaging plus more software, hardware infrastructure and partnerships.

CEO Elliott Potter says Linq's goal is to help developers build customer-facing agents that "use human tools to do human work."
