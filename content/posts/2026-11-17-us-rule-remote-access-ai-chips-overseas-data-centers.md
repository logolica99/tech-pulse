On November 16, the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) released an interim final rule aimed at a gap in chip export controls. Until now, companies in China and other countries of concern could avoid importing restricted chips by renting compute from data centers in third countries. BIS says the rule brings remote access in line with the controls that already apply to physical shipments. The agency also says the rule was written by and at the direction of the Treasury Department and the National Security Council.

## What changes

- **Data center end-use control.** Exporters need a license to ship advanced chips and servers to any data center, anywhere, if they know it provides more than 12 million "aggregate TPP" of compute to companies headquartered in China, Macau, or other Country Group D:5 destinations. The threshold can be met across linked data centers.
- **Know-your-customer duties.** Exporters are expected to get information about the foreign data center's own customer due diligence. If a data center refuses to share it, that counts as a red flag.
- **Five new license countries.** Advanced chips going to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil now need a license. Shipments to US-headquartered companies are exempt if the exporter files monthly reports.
- **Existing arrangements.** Data centers that already serve such customers at scale have 90 days after the rule takes effect to obtain a license.
- **Carve-out.** Commercial recommender systems used only to rank or suggest products or content are excluded. Any generative system is not.

The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule, which was never enforced, and tightens how chip foundries and packagers treat certain chip designers.

## Timing

According to Nixon Peabody's analysis, the rule is expected to be published in the Federal Register and take effect on November 19. Comments will be due 60 days after that.
