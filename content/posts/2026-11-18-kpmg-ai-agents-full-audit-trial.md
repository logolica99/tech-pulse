KPMG has tested what happens when AI agents handle an entire company audit instead of single chores. According to the Financial Times, as reported by Business Standard, the Big Four firm deployed more than 100 AI agents on the financial statements of an unnamed FTSE 250 company this year, running the experiment in parallel with a conventional audit by its own staff.

## How the trial worked

The project, codenamed Project Exponential, used agents at every stage of the audit. They gathered and analysed data, documented their work, drafted conclusions and observations, coordinated with each other on their own, and proposed next steps. A team built the agents over four months.

People stayed in the loop: staff set the agents' goals, briefed them on the company's business and earlier audits, reviewed their output, and challenged their conclusions. An audit partner signed off on the experiment, but KPMG did not rely on its results when it formally approved the company's accounts.

## What KPMG found

Cath Burnet, KPMG's UK head of audit, said that in several cases the AI went "so much deeper" than the regular team and asked "better questions." It was not flawless: an auditor had to step in when an agent failed to match a set of regulations to the company's documents.

KPMG plans to extend the approach to as many as 20 larger audits and eventually offer AI-based audits to clients. It said the technology is not yet ready to run thousands of audits, that some companies' data is not clean enough for the agents, and it would not say whether time savings would mean lower fees. Rival PwC is running its own parallel audit tests, and the UK's Financial Reporting Council has warned about risks to auditor independence when AI-generated insights are shared with clients.
