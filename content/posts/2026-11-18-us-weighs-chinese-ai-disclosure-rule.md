The Trump administration is considering a rule that would make US companies say publicly when they rely on Chinese AI models, Reuters reported on November 18, citing three people familiar with the discussions.

## What is on the table

According to Reuters, the Treasury Department and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy have discussed the idea. Companies such as Airbnb, which uses Alibaba's Qwen model for its AI customer-service agent, would have to post that fact on their websites. The approach would try to discourage reliance on Chinese open-weight models through disclosure rather than outright restrictions. Reuters said it was not clear whether the plan is gaining traction or would become a formal rule.

Airbnb told Reuters its AI work runs overwhelmingly on US-origin models and that the few Chinese-origin models it uses are open source and run through approved US-based providers. China's embassy in Washington said Beijing opposes politicizing tech issues and would protect Chinese companies' rights. The White House, Treasury and Alibaba did not respond to Reuters.

## Why now

Chinese open-weight models such as Kimi, DeepSeek and Qwen have spread among US companies because they are cheaper than proprietary US models and perform well enough. Reuters notes Chinese models were the three most-used by tokens on OpenRouter in October. House committee chairs Andrew Garbarino and John Moolenaar have already been investigating US firms' use of Chinese AI, and Moolenaar welcomed the administration's deliberations as an important step.
