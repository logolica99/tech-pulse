Ford has begun producing lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery cells in the United States using technology licensed from Chinese battery maker CATL, CNBC reports.

## The plant

Cell production at Ford's $3 billion plant in Marshall, Michigan, about 100 miles west of Detroit, started last month, and the company said Wednesday that cells are now shipping. The first cells go into prototypes of the Fathom, Ford's upcoming small electric pickup, which is expected to launch in the fourth quarter of 2027.

The roughly 2 million-square-foot facility is designed to turn out 40 cells a minute, or about 20 gigawatt-hours a year for EVs and Ford's energy storage business. That is half the up to 40 GWh originally planned, a cut made as Ford and the wider industry pulled back on EVs. Ford says it is still certifying its processes and plans to ramp production next year.

## Why CATL

LFP chemistry was invented in the U.S. but commercialized largely in China, and it is cheaper than the nickel-based cells common in American EVs. Ford's Alan Clarke told reporters that licensing from CATL was the fastest way to bring low-cost LFP production to the U.S. and learn to make the cells in-house.

The CATL tie has drawn criticism from some lawmakers and from Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, who wrote to CEO Jim Farley in September expressing concern about Ford's links to Chinese companies.
