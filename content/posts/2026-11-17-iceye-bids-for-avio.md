ICEYE, the Finnish company best known for its fleet of synthetic-aperture radar satellites, wants to buy Avio, the Italian launch and propulsion firm behind the Vega rocket. ICEYE's board approved an all-cash voluntary tender offer, made through an Italian subsidiary called Iris 2, of €41.00 for every Avio share. Full acceptance would put the deal at roughly €2.05 billion.

According to ICEYE, the price is a 21.3% premium to Avio's closing price on November 16, and about 30% and 35% above its one-month and three-month volume-weighted averages.

## Why a satellite company wants a rocket company

ICEYE's pitch is vertical integration: one European company that builds satellites, launches them and runs the missions. The company argues that would give ICEYE steadier access to launch, give Avio clearer visibility into future demand so it can invest in production and fly Vega more often, and give governments more certainty over deploying and maintaining sovereign space systems.

ICEYE says Italy would stay the center of Avio's launch and propulsion business, that it would keep the Avio brand and workforce, and that it plans to invest an extra €1 billion in Italy over five years after the deal closes, which it expects to create up to 400 skilled jobs.

## What happens next

ICEYE plans to pay with cash on its balance sheet plus acquisition debt. The offer still needs regulatory approvals and must meet the other conditions set out in its formal notice under Italian market rules, and a full offer document is still to come.
