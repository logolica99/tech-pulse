Automotive plastics supplier Novares has put a humanoid robot to work on a real production floor. At its factory in Seseña, Spain, a Unitree G1-D humanoid picked and placed plastic parts, packed them and handled boxes alongside human workers. Systems integrator Innov8 and software company Bleu Robotics ran the trial with Novares.

## The numbers

Novares reports a 100% detection rate for parts, 99.5% success at picking and placing them, and 95% success at box handling. The robot completed 200 autonomous cycles in one hour and moved around the work area on its own. Innov8 says the robot learned the job from operator demonstrations: twelve demonstrations and eleven minutes of training. These figures come from the companies and have not been independently checked.

## Still a proof of concept

The on-site trial started in early November, after four months of testing at Novares' R&D center in Lens, France. Novares calls it a proof of concept, not a permanent deployment. As Rockingrobots notes, the company has not shared data needed to judge full-scale use, such as performance over full shifts, downtime, failures, human interventions or cost.
