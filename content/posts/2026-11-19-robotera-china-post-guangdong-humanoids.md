ROBOTERA, a Beijing humanoid robot maker, says its robots are now working inside China Post's Guangdong network at 11 postal hubs spread over eight cities: Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Dongguan, Jiangmen, Shantou, Shanwei, Jieyang and Heyuan. The company announced the rollout on November 17 and calls it the largest and most geographically spread deployment of embodied-AI robots in logistics so far.

## What the robots do

The robots handle parcel induction, the repetitive job of feeding packages onto sorting lines. Guangdong moves more express parcels than any other Chinese province, so it is a tough real-world test. ROBOTERA says its setup can reject irregular items, adjust its speed to avoid jams and missed parcels, and uses the company's own five-finger dexterous hand. Human staff shift toward robot maintenance, coordination and quality control.

The project grew out of a pilot that started in March 2026 at the Guangzhou Postal District Center. According to the company, the robots there average more than 1,500 parcels per hour, which it says is comparable to an experienced human worker. ROBOTERA also says the system is already used in postal operations in East China.

## A number worth watching

Robrief points out that the 1,500-per-hour figure has shifted: the company described it as a Double 11 peak a week earlier, and a May report from the same Guangzhou hub cited a maximum of 1,200 per hour. Robrief also notes, citing Huanqiu, that China Post's Hangzhou hub has 45 of the robots and that ROBOTERA has delivered several hundred units to logistics customers. Neither the company nor those reports gave a robot count for Guangdong, and figures like uptime and how often humans had to step in were not disclosed.
