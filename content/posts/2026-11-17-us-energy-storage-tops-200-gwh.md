The United States now has more than 200 GWh of energy storage installed, according to the Q4 2026 US Energy Storage Market Outlook from the Solar Energy Industries Association (SEIA) and Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, released on November 17 at the RE+ conference in Las Vegas.

## The numbers

- **Q3 2026:** 14.3 GWh of new storage came online.
- **First nine months of 2026:** 48.3 GWh, which the groups say is up 20% from the same period last year.
- **Mix:** Utility-scale projects made up most of the quarter at 12.2 GWh. Commercial and industrial systems added 1.3 GWh and homes added 746 MWh.
- **States:** Through Q3, Texas led utility-scale deployment with 12.3 GWh, ahead of Arizona at 7.7 GWh and California at 6.8 GWh. Eleven states, including Colorado, Massachusetts, Michigan, Georgia and Illinois, have already set new annual installation records with a quarter still to go.

The report also raises its forecast for cumulative US deployment by 2030 by 3.7%, to 708 GWh across grid-scale and behind-the-meter systems.

## Factories and policy

SEIA says US battery cell manufacturing capacity has reached 92.4 GWh, a 50% jump, after new cell plants opened in Tennessee in July and Indiana in October.

The industry group is using the report to push Congress to pass the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, a permitting bill that more than 300 solar and storage companies backed in a letter earlier this month. SEIA argues that more storage is helping hold down power prices. It points to its own analysis showing average wholesale electricity prices fell 42% in Texas and 51% in California between summer 2021 and summer 2026, though that claim comes from an industry advocate.
