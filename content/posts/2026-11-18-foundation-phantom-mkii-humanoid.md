San Francisco startup Foundation Future Industries has shown off Phantom MKII, its second-generation humanoid robot, along with Cortex II, the AI system that runs it. The company is pitching the machine for construction sites, factories, and defense work.

## What's new

According to CEO Sankaet Pathak, Phantom MKII can walk while carrying up to 200 pounds, compared with roughly 50 to 60 pounds for the first version, and can keep working in rain, snow, and dust. Other company claims include:

- Weather-resistant joints and modular parts that can be swapped in the field.
- Up to six hours of battery life, less when hauling heavy loads.
- Redesigned actuators with more than 1.5 times the previous performance, in joints about three-quarters the old size.
- A bill of materials cut roughly in half.

Cortex II is designed to learn physical tasks by watching people do them, in some cases from as few as 60 demonstrations, the company says. Foundation is targeting the start of production in early 2027 in the San Francisco Bay Area, beginning with several hundred robots and aiming for a few thousand by the end of that year. These figures come from the company and have not been independently tested.

## The weapons question

Foundation, which counts Eric Trump among its investors and advisers, has been open about military uses. Pathak told Fast Company the company is "not anti-weaponization and warfare" and would consider arming its robots if the U.S. military asked, arguing that ground robots could mean fewer drone and air strikes. For now, he says, most of its defense work is about logistics. He also disputed a Financial Times report that the company was in talks to supply up to 1,000 first-generation robots to the Israel Defense Forces, describing only very early exploratory conversations.
