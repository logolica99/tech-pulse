Palo Alto Networks said on November 18 that it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Applied Compute, a startup whose platform handles post-training and inference for open-weight AI models. The all-stock deal is valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments, and still needs regulatory clearance. Palo Alto expects it to close in its fiscal third quarter of 2027.

## What Applied Compute does

Applied Compute helps companies take openly released models, tune them on their own data, and run them. Palo Alto's pitch is that enterprise AI will be hybrid, with many companies running open-weight models alongside closed frontier models, and that nobody large is yet offering those open models with security built in. It plans to let customers train, adapt and deploy them in public clouds or sovereign environments, and to use the Applied Compute research team to train its own large and small models.

According to BankInfoSecurity, the company was founded in May 2025, has about 50 employees, and earlier this month passed a $100 million annualized revenue run rate and closed a $350 million round at a $3.25 billion valuation. CEO Yash Patil spent more than two years on OpenAI's technical staff; Palo Alto says he will become its Co-Chief AI Officer once the deal closes.

## The bigger picture

The deal came alongside Palo Alto's quarterly results. Reuters, via CNA, reported first-quarter revenue of $3.45 billion, up 39% from a year earlier, a raised fiscal 2027 revenue forecast of $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion, and an after-hours share jump of about 8.5%. BankInfoSecurity notes it would be Palo Alto's second-largest acquisition ever, after its $25 billion purchase of CyberArk earlier this year, and reports that Palo Alto expects Applied Compute to add more than $1 billion in next-generation security annual recurring revenue by 2029.
