Chinese perovskite maker MicroQuanta Semiconductor and Zhejiang University report a certified steady-state efficiency of 23.9% for a 19.48 cm² perovskite mini-module, which they describe as a world record for that size. China's National PV Industry Measurement and Testing Center at the Fujian Metrology Institute certified the result.

## Why modules matter

Perovskite cells post high efficiencies in the lab, but they usually lose ground when scaled up into modules. The team analysed which parameters drive that drop and found that fill factor, not voltage or current, was the biggest contributor once losses from laser-scribed inactive areas were accounted for.

## The fix: a quick chill

To tackle it, the researchers cooled the freshly coated wet perovskite film to 0–10 °C for about 15 seconds before vacuum treatment. That slowed crystallization and produced larger, more uniform grains. The slot-die-coated module, made of eight series-connected subcells, reached a fill factor of 83.3%, which the authors say is the highest reported for a perovskite module, with 97% of its aperture area active.

The cooling step also seemed to help durability. In continuous one-sun aging tests, the efficiency loss tied to falling fill factor was less than a third of that seen in control modules. The work was published in *Nature Communications*.

MicroQuanta is also building a gigawatt-scale perovskite production line in Quzhou, Zhejiang, aimed at 2.88 m² modules.
