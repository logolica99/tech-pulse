Jensen Huang has put his name forward to get his old job back. In a written statement obtained by CNBC on Tuesday, his first public comment since he resigned as Nvidia's CEO last week, the company's co-founder apologized and said he would return if the board wanted him.

## What Huang said

Huang wrote that he "did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA" and had "made serious mistakes" in his personal life. He said he regrets the problems he created for the board and will cooperate with its independent investigation.

He also said employees, investors, partners and customers have been asking him to come back. "If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO," the statement says.

CNBC notes that an online petition for his return has passed 180,000 signatures and that President Donald Trump said on Monday he is open to it. Mint reports that investors Brad Gerstner of Altimeter and Gavin Baker of Atreides urged the board to consider his return, and that Elon Musk has backed the idea.

## What the board is doing

The board does not appear to be moving. CNBC's sources said on Monday that it is not considering bringing Huang back, and board chair Harvey Jones said the same on Tuesday's earnings call. Citing The Wall Street Journal, Mint reports that Nvidia has hired executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent CEO and will look at both internal and outside candidates. CFO Colette Kress is running the company on an interim basis.

Huang stepped down on November 12 after disclosures about alleged relationships, including one with a woman alleged to have contact with Chinese intelligence. CNBC reports that Nvidia has hired law firm Wachtell Lipton to investigate his conduct and whether it compromised national security or company secrets.
