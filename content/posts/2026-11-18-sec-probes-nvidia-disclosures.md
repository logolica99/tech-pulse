The US Securities and Exchange Commission has opened a civil investigation into Nvidia that includes whether the company's disclosures misled investors, according to The Wall Street Journal, as reported by TechCrunch and the AP. The full scope of the probe is not clear, and the Journal did not establish whether any specific reporting decision is under review.

## The Singapore question

The report puts fresh focus on a change Nvidia made in 2025. According to Fortune, in the quarter ending July 2025 Nvidia reported $10.2 billion in revenue billed to Singapore, about 22% of its total. In the next quarter, Singapore disappeared from its geographic breakdown after Nvidia switched to grouping revenue by where customers are headquartered instead of where they are billed.

That change came around the time Nvidia added Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China, to an internal block list, per the Journal's earlier reporting cited by Fortune. The Commerce Department has been investigating Megaspeed since late 2024, and Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing.

## Pressure from several directions

The probe lands days after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO following disclosures of extramarital affairs, including one with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom US intelligence officials reportedly believe works for Chinese intelligence. The AP says the Justice Department is leading multiple investigations involving her, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has asked whether Nvidia pushed sales into China to boost results, saying he wants answers from the board and outside counsel within 10 days.

The news broke as Nvidia reported a $115.8 billion quarter, up 103% from a year earlier, to a market that has pushed its shares down more than 10% since Huang's departure.
