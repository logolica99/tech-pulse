President Donald Trump said on Friday that the US will not allow American companies to build data centers in other countries, and that any company that does will face a "tremendous tax".

Speaking at a White House press conference after his summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, Trump compared the issue to chips, saying letting that industry move abroad had been "very destructive" to the US. He warned that if America lets data centers go elsewhere, "China will take them all."

## No details yet

Trump gave no tax rate, legal basis or timeline. He said the idea was so new that he had not yet told his own staff. The Verge notes it could be hard to pass with a new Democratic-majority Congress being sworn in within a couple of months.

## Why it matters

Big US cloud companies, including Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle, already run data centers in other countries, often to sit close to customers or meet local rules, CNBC reports. At the same time, data centers are unpopular at home: in a CNBC survey, 64% of registered voters opposed an AI data center within 10 miles of where they live, and some states and cities have added moratoriums or new restrictions. Trump's threat appears aimed at making overseas building less attractive as domestic resistance grows.
