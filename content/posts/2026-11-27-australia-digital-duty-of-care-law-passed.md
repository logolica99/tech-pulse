Australia's Parliament has passed the Digital Duty of Care, a new online safety law that puts the burden on platforms to prevent serious harm, the federal communications department said on November 27.

## Who it covers

The duty applies to online services broadly, including games, apps, social media platforms and AI chatbots. Services must protect Australians from seriously harmful material and conduct, and make sure their own features and tools don't cause harm. They also have to protect under-18s from harmful content, conduct and features, with pornography, eating disorder material and online bullying and abuse named as examples.

## Other pieces

- **Feed choice:** The minister will be able to make "My Feed My Way" rules requiring social platforms to let users pick between recommended content and only content from accounts they follow.
- **eSafety powers:** The eSafety Commissioner gets expanded powers, including on cyberbullying, abuse and the creation of fake nude images.
- **Penalties:** Breaches can bring fines of up to 5% of global revenue or A$109.2 million, whichever is greater. Individuals and groups will also be able to sue over breaches.

The law takes effect 12 months after it receives Royal Assent.
