Boltz, an AI lab building foundation models for biology and chemistry, has raised $110 million in a Series A round led by Bessemer Venture Partners. Bessemer's Andrew Hedin and Byron Deeter join the company, and existing backers Andreessen Horowitz, Amplify Partners, Zetta Venture Partners and Obvious Ventures all invested again. New investors include Greycroft, F-Prime Capital, Hummingbird and Benchling.

## From open-source models to a platform

The founders came out of Regina Barzilay's lab at MIT, where they built the open-source Boltz models for protein structure prediction and binding. Bessemer says those models have passed 5 million downloads and are used by more than 100,000 scientists.

Boltz spun out in 2025, launched its Boltz Lab platform in January and released its first proprietary models, BoltzMol-1 for small molecules and BoltzProt-1 for protein design, in June. The company says it now works with six of the world's 20 largest drugmakers, including Pfizer, GSK, Takeda and BMS, and that the volume of molecular tokens processed for paying users has grown 50-fold since June. Those are company-reported figures.

## What the money is for

Boltz says it will spend the funding on more compute and data, on meeting partner demand and on growing its team of about 40. Its long-term goal is to let a scientist go from a therapeutic idea to a molecule ready for human testing largely on a computer, though any AI-designed candidate still has to prove itself in the lab and the clinic.
