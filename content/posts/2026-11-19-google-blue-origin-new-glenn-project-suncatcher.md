Google has picked Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket for the first dedicated heavy-lift launch of Project Suncatcher, its long-term research effort to run machine learning compute on solar-powered satellites in orbit. Both companies announced the launch services agreement on November 19.

## What's planned

- **Target date:** the first quarter of 2029.
- **Payload:** Suncatcher satellites carrying Google's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs). Each satellite is meant to have roughly the computing capacity of one data center rack, according to both announcements.
- **Orbit:** sun-synchronous, which gives the satellites near-constant sunlight for power.

Google describes Suncatcher as a "moonshot": the idea is that an interconnected network of these satellites could scale AI compute using the plentiful solar energy available in space. Travis Beals, who leads the project at Google, called the dedicated launch a crucial milestone on that roadmap. Google says the launch adds to its existing work with other satellite and launch partners.

For Blue Origin, it's another commercial customer for New Glenn. The company pointed to the rocket's heavy-lift capacity and large payload volume as the reason it suits missions like this one.

## Why it matters

Suncatcher has been a research project; this agreement gives it a specific rocket and a target window for putting rack-scale AI hardware into orbit. Neither announcement disclosed financial terms.
