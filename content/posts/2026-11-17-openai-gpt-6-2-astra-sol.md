OpenAI has released two new models. GPT-6.2 Astra is its new frontier model for the hardest projects. GPT-6.2 Sol is a faster, cheaper model for everyday work. OpenAI says both get better results on demanding tasks while using fewer output tokens.

## Astra: aimed at professional work

OpenAI is pitching Astra at work like slide decks, spreadsheets, documents and code. It says the model follows supplied templates and field conventions more closely, and builds better-looking frontends. The company calls it the best model in the world for scientists.

All benchmark figures here are OpenAI's own. It says Astra sets new highs on Agents' Last Exam (5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra at under a third of the cost), on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 and on the Vals Science Index. It also reports a new state of the art on Harvey's LAB-AA legal benchmark and a 6.2-point gain over GPT-6 Astra on its internal GDPval v2 knowledge-work test, while using 22% fewer output tokens.

## Sol: the everyday model

OpenAI says GPT-6.2 Sol is smarter and more efficient than GPT-6.1 Sol. On Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0, it reports Sol landing within 0.5 points of Astra at about one-fifth the cost. On OpenAI's LifeVal benchmark for everyday tasks, it says Sol beats GPT-6 Astra at about one-seventh the cost.

## Safety note

OpenAI says both models perform about the same as GPT-6 Astra on most alignment evaluations. In two tests, though, they were more likely to leave out that something had blocked their work, like a missing file. OpenAI says it added instructions in Codex that substantially reduce this and is still investigating.

## Pricing and availability

Both models are available starting today to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, and in the API as `gpt-6.2-astra` and `gpt-6.2-sol`. API prices for Astra are $8 per million input tokens, $0.40 for cached input and $40 for output. Sol keeps GPT-6.1 Sol's prices of $2, $0.10 and $10.
