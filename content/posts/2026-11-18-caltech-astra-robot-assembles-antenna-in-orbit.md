A robotic arm has put together a structure in orbit entirely on its own, according to reporting by New Scientist cited by VICE.

## What happened

Caltech aerospace engineer Sergio Pellegrino and his team, backed by a DARPA initiative on new orbital manufacturing techniques, launched a robotic arm system called ASTRA, short for Autonomous In-Space Tensioned Reflector Assembly, from California. Once in orbit, it assembled a 1.4-meter radio antenna out of composite tubes and joints, with no instructions from astronauts.

## Why it matters

Building in orbit has so far relied on astronauts, mostly for relatively small jobs. Large structures such as giant solar arrays or habitats won't fit inside a launch vehicle's cargo bay, and having people assemble them in space is dangerous, slow, and expensive. Robots that can do the assembly themselves are one way around that.

It is an early step. ASTRA isn't making materials in orbit; it is assembling prebuilt parts, more like snapping together a complex kit than manufacturing. But it shows an autonomous robot can complete a real structure in space.
