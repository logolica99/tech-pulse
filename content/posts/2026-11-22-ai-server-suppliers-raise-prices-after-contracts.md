Makers of AI servers are going back to customers before delivery and asking for substantially more money than the prices discussed when contracts were signed months ago, according to a Financial Times report cited by the Times of India. Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro are among the suppliers named.

## Memory is the main driver

The suppliers are largely passing on higher memory chip costs, the report says. It cites JPMorgan research showing memory prices up more than 400% since the start of 2024. One person familiar with the matter said GPU server prices have risen at least 20% to 30%, and another described increases of 25%, 30% and 45% as "disconcerting." With demand for AI hardware outrunning supply, buyers have few alternatives when their orders are ready to ship.

## A court fight in Ireland

The report points to Irish court filings in a dispute between Dell and London trading firm XTX Markets, which sued in June. According to the filings, the two had agreed a fixed price of $549 million for servers, and XTX paid another $5 million for price certainty. XTX alleges Dell later said rising component costs meant it would need at least $62 million to $70 million more, and that it could not absorb the higher memory prices.

Other trading firms building their own data centers, including Jane Street, Optiver and Hudson River Trading, are also facing increases, according to the report. Some contracts only set an indicative price at signing, and buyers contacted by the FT would not say whether their deals allowed for later changes. The trading firms have mostly agreed to pay because they need the hardware.
