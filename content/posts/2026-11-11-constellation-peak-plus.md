Constellation Energy is selling a contract it calls Peak+ to large power buyers, including the data-center operators looking for round-the-clock supply. Utility Dive reported the pitch on November 11, 2026, from the company's third-quarter earnings call. The American Nuclear Society's Nuclear Newswire followed the next day.

Chief executive Joe Dominguez described Peak+ as capacity solutions plus the existing clean fleet. In practice that fleet means nuclear. The extra capacity, he said, can include batteries, new generation, and demand response. He told analysts the company could add batteries and generation on land it already owns around its plants, and he presented the product as a simpler alternative to building a new gas plant. Utility Dive noted the risks he cited for new plants: construction delays, cost overruns, interconnection delays, and uncertain fuel prices.

## What the first contract actually covers

The first signed agreement is narrower than that menu. Both outlets quote Constellation's release: a 20-year deal for 270 megawatts of unit-contingent energy, capacity, and attributes from the existing nuclear fleet. Nuclear Newswire says deliveries start in 2028. Dominguez did not name the customer. He gave pairing a customer with a battery storage provider as an example of how Peak+ can be structured. Neither report says batteries are included in this 270 megawatt contract.

A slide in the investor presentation, described by Utility Dive, prices Peak+ at $20 to $50 per megawatt-hour above the floor set by the federal nuclear production tax credit. The same slide puts new combined-cycle gas in eastern PJM at $110 to $130 per megawatt-hour by 2032, assuming a build cost of $3,000 per kilowatt. Constellation says the production tax credit pays nuclear units up to $15 per megawatt-hour when revenue falls between $26 and $44.75 per megawatt-hour.

Utility Dive puts the company's fleet at about 55 gigawatts, roughly 22 gigawatts of it nuclear, much of that inside PJM. Peak+ sits next to a separate effort to uprate existing reactors. Uprates add megawatts at the plant. The first Peak+ contract sells output from reactors that are already running, with room in the product design for new capacity alongside that output.
