Nomadic, a Munich energy-tech startup founded in 2024, has raised $6 million in seed funding to grow its fleet of drones that monitor high-voltage power lines from on top of the wires themselves. Foundamental led the round, joined by Verve Ventures, Cyan Ventures, existing investors Outlander VC and Asia2G Capital, and angels from the sdw alumni network.

## How it works

The company's NOMAD drones weigh about four kilograms and land directly on live conductors, with no outage needed. They recharge inductively from the line and can move between sections to wherever an operator wants measurements. Nomadic says each drone can cover about 25 kilometres of line, compared with fixed sensors that are typically installed every one to five kilometres.

The data feeds into Nomadic OS, which combines it with weather models, grid data and historical records so operators can spot developing problems and see how much capacity their lines really have.

## Track record and plans

According to the company, the drones already run on German transmission and distribution grids and have been deployed in Norway and the United States, logging more than 20,000 hours on live lines at temperatures down to minus 25 °C and voltages up to 420 kV.

With the new money, Nomadic plans to bring remote drone relocation into production with customers in 2027, expand deployments with grid operators in Europe and the US, and hire in Munich.
