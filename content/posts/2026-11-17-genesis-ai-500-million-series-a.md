Genesis AI, a Silicon Valley startup building AI models for robot hands, has raised a $500 million Series A led by Khosla Ventures, Premji Invest, and Eclipse, TechCrunch reported. The company was founded in 2024 by Zhou Xian and Théophile Gervet, who met as graduate students at Carnegie Mellon, and announced a $105 million seed round in July 2025.

## The bet: dexterity through data

Genesis is vertically integrated: it gathers most of its own training data, trains its own model, and designs its own humanoid robot. Its data comes from sensor-equipped gloves and headset cameras worn by more than 100 workers around the world, capturing force information the company believes is key to reliable manipulation. The robots wear the same gloves, and their hands are built to human proportions. The startup also leans heavily on physics simulation to generate large numbers of training scenarios.

In a demo for TechCrunch, the model drove a pair of off-the-shelf robot arms through a packing task: boxing two items, taping the box shut, and applying a label. On a second run the robot fumbled a box and struggled with the label, a reminder of the gap between lab demos and dependable automation.

## Eno and what comes next

Genesis says it has built 30 to 40 units of Eno, its flagship humanoid, is piloting it with a handful of undisclosed customers, and has picked a contract manufacturer to begin mass production in the US next year. The CEO named hardware as the main bottleneck: the company's dexterous hands currently last only a few weeks of regular work before needing replacement.
