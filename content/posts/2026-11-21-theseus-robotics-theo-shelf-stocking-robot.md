Theseus Robotics, the first spinoff from the ETH Robotics Club in Zurich, is launching its Theo robot in its first US and EU grocery stores this November, according to Startupticker. The startup recently received a $500,000 investment from Y Combinator.

## What Theo does

Theo targets the last, mostly manual step of grocery logistics: getting products from the back room onto shelves. The company says the robot can restock up to 400 units an hour and pick up to 40 units an hour for online orders. In a typical day it picks online orders until 9 p.m., then switches to restocking overnight until the store opens at 9 a.m.

Force sensors help it handle fragile items like yoghurt cups, and it checks shelves against planograms, the layouts retailers use to decide where products go, so stores can see gaps in real time.

## Software first

Theseus says it is focusing on the robot's software rather than its hardware. "Robot hardware is turning into a commodity, so we build the brain," CEO Samarth Malhotra said. The robot runs an operating system and models the startup trains itself, and the team tests it in simulation across thousands of products, plus a demo supermarket near Zurich where they deliberately move products and bump shelves.
