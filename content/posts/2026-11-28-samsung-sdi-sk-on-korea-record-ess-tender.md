South Korea's climate and energy ministry has picked the projects for its third central contract market for energy storage systems (ESS), and two domestic battery makers took all of it.

## The results

- **1,194 MW** went to 14 sites for priority negotiation, more than the first two rounds combined (1,128 MW). The round was announced at 1,180 MW, but the government allowed awards up to 105%.
- **Samsung SDI** won 751 MW, about 63%, supplying nine sites: seven on the mainland and two on Jeju. Its total across all three rounds is now 1,380 MW.
- **SK On** won 443 MW, about 37%, at five mainland sites, taking it to 727 MW in total. The Herald Business reports that a developer that used to work with LG Energy Solution switched four sites, 384 MW, to SK On.
- **LG Energy Solution** did not bid, saying it would focus on contracts it already has.

MoneyToday puts the round at about 7.08 GWh of storage, and the industry values it at around 2 trillion won ($1.49 billion). Non-price factors such as fire safety and contribution to the domestic economy carried more weight this time.

Most of the capacity, 1,019 MW, is in the southwestern Honam region, where solar output is highest. Final volumes are due on December 4. Projects must have generation licences by August 2027 and be built by February 2029. SK On plans to start deliveries in the first half of 2027 from a converted line at its Seosan plant. A fourth round is planned for the first half of 2027.
