The European Commission is getting ready to recommend a fivefold increase in the European Competitiveness Fund allocation that supports space, to €125 billion (about $137 billion), Via Satellite reports. The aim is to back the European space sector across the whole innovation lifecycle, from research to growing companies.

Maria Fernández Molinero, head of the space policy unit at the Commission's Directorate-General for Defence Industry and Space, made the case at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen on November 18. She said keeping the status quo "is not enough for the decade we see ahead of us." She added that Europe has to turn strong space technology into sustainable businesses, and that access to capital is key to doing so.

## Where the money would go

Molinero listed three priorities:

- **Access to space**, which she said specifically means launchers.
- **Space commercialization** and the wider space economy.
- **Technological sovereignty** through research and innovation.

She said negotiations on the fund are still going on and should be finalized soon, so the figure is a proposal, not an approved budget.

## Space Act and a "Space Shield"

Molinero also said the proposed EU Space Act would make the rules clearer and more predictable than today's patchwork of national regimes. She added that the Commission is preparing an action plan for a European Space Shield. It would protect European satellites and services through surveillance and tracking, security monitoring, secure communications, resilient ground stations, and AI-enabled detection, with industry involved from the start.
