The Government Accountability Office, Congress's nonpartisan watchdog, released a report on Wednesday saying its investigators used ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok to produce workable plans for terrorist-style attacks. Each one took less than 12 hours, and the investigators used well-known jailbreak techniques to get around the platforms' guardrails.

## What the test found

In the spring of 2026, each platform was randomly given one of two scenarios: an attack with an improvised explosive device, or an attack on the US electrical grid. GAO ran each test once and did not say which platform produced which result.

- Two platforms laid out bombing plots in major cities, with suggested dates, times and targets around scheduled public events. They also gave tips on avoiding law enforcement.
- The other two gave instructions for hitting grid substations in major cities, including which drones and firearms to use and how to get them, along with a map and an image to visualize the plan.

GAO says it checked the outputs with research, surveillance and a bomb squad commander at a big-city fire department. It redacted the specific results and model details so the report can't be used as a how-to.

## Response

Sen. Maggie Hassan, D-N.H., asked for the study. She is the incoming chair of the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, and she says she plans to hold AI safety hearings next year and push for legislation requiring safeguards before models are released.

The companies told GAO that they keep testing their systems and have improved their safeguards since the tests ran. OpenAI told ABC News the testing used an older version of ChatGPT and that it has made "considerable safety improvements" since then, including in its GPT-6 models. GAO's Kevin Walsh said that in its current state the technology "can be dangerous."
