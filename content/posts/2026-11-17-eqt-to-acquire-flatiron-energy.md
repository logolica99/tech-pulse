Investment firm EQT said on November 17 that its EQT Transition Infrastructure strategy has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Flatiron Energy, a developer and operator of utility-scale battery storage in the eastern United States, from private equity firm Hull Street Energy. Financial terms were not disclosed.

## What Flatiron has built

Flatiron was founded in 2021 and builds, owns and operates storage projects in New England, New York and the Mid-Atlantic power markets, targeting dense, capacity-constrained areas where batteries can help at times of peak demand. According to EQT's announcement, the company has grown its portfolio nearly tenfold since Hull Street first invested in 2022, to 24 GWh. It has won 6 GWh of long-term contracts and has 2 GWh of batteries under construction, which EQT says includes the largest urban energy storage project in the world.

## Why storage, why now

EQT points to rising electricity demand, retiring power plants and more renewable generation coming online in these markets. In its announcement, the firm projects that storage capacity in Flatiron's regions will grow from 2 GWh operating in 2026 to 108 GWh by 2035.

EQT says it already owns battery storage businesses in Europe, including Statera Energy in the UK and Ju:niz Energy in Germany, and plans to invest in Flatiron's team as it delivers its contracted projects and moves into new markets.

The deal still needs customary approvals and is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026.
