Nvidia's top Blackwell server hardware has shown up in paperwork for a data center in Shanghai, according to a Bloomberg investigation summarized by TechSpot and Tom's Hardware. Blackwell chips cannot be shipped to China without a US export licence.

## What the filings show

The records are loan documents filed with Chinese regulators for the Yangtze River Delta Project, a site local media say is meant to hold up to 800,000 servers.

- **Shanghai Shuoyao** pledged a GB300 NVL72 rack, one of Nvidia's most powerful systems, as collateral for a 96 million yuan (about $14 million) loan from Shanghai Innovation Bank. The filing, from November, includes the rack's serial number.
- **Shanghai Kezhan** listed a GB200 server in documents for a 90 million yuan loan it took in late 2025 from a finance-leasing firm owned by a Shanghai government agency. Kezhan also disclosed 32 servers with H200 chips at the site.
- **Shanghai Longjie**, which shares a parent with Shuoyao, reported Hopper-generation servers from Super Micro and Dell as collateral for a 200 million yuan loan.

A public warranty database shows the GB300 rack was built by Gigabyte. Gigabyte declined to say who first bought it, citing company policy.

## The Megaspeed link

The site is tied to Alice Huang, who founded Singapore cloud firm Megaspeed in 2023. Megaspeed became Nvidia's biggest customer in Southeast Asia, is now under US investigation over possible chip smuggling, and was put on a US trade blacklist this month. It denies wrongdoing. Shuoyao says its relationship with Megaspeed is "ordinary commercial cooperation." Kezhan's ownership traces back to a Tokyo firm that Caixin reported is controlled by Huang.

There is no evidence that Megaspeed itself shipped hardware to the site, and how the rack reached China is unknown. Customs data does show an $11 million shipment flown from Malaysia to Shanghai for Kezhan in May 2025, labelled "storage chassis" but priced like Hopper servers. Nvidia did not respond to questions.
