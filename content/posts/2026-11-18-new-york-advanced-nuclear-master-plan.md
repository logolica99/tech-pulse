New York now has a written roadmap for its nuclear ambitions. Governor Kathy Hochul announced on Wednesday that the state has completed its Master Plan for Responsible Advanced Nuclear Development, unveiled at New York's third Nuclear Summit in Rochester.

## What the plan is for

The plan is meant to deliver what the governor calls the Nuclear Reliability Backbone: 5 gigawatts of new nuclear generation on top of the 3.4 gigawatts the state already has, for a total of about 8.4 GW. The state describes it as what would be the largest nuclear buildout in the US in more than three decades.

It was co-written by the state energy research agency NYSERDA and the Department of Public Service, and according to the announcement it contains 30 action points backed by 12 studies and more than 1,000 pages of analysis.

## What is in it

The focus areas listed by the state include:

- a policy framework to cut costs and make projects commercially viable;
- building on federal efforts such as Project Janus at Fort Drum;
- a supplier readiness program and a longer-term nuclear supply chain strategy;
- retraining existing workers and aligning specialized training with actual deployment;
- a process for finding interested host communities and suitable sites, including brownfields;
- reviewing state permitting rules while keeping safety requirements intact.

## Alongside the plan

The Department of Public Service also issued a white paper with policy recommendations for the Public Service Commission, including a proposed package of support policies aimed at making projects financially workable.

Separately, the New York Power Authority is still working on its own project of at least 1 GW upstate. The state says eight communities have expressed interest in hosting a plant, and that NYPA qualified ten nuclear developers as potential partners this month. NYSERDA plans a public webinar on the master plan on January 13, 2027.
