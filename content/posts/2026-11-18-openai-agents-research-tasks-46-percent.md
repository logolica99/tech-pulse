OpenAI published its second report on how much AI is speeding up its own research. The headline number: its coding agents completed an estimated 46% of delegated research tasks that would take a person two to four hours, with no human intervention, in October. In March that figure was 23%.

For longer jobs, estimated at four to eight hours of human work, the zero-intervention success rate rose from 13% to 35% over the same period.

## A jagged frontier

The gains are uneven, which is where the report gets its title. OpenAI says agents succeeded on their own 79% of the time when monitoring training runs for problems and 72% of the time answering technical questions, but only 35% when launching training runs, 29% when building evaluation and reinforcement-learning environments, and 25% when writing code for inference and serving stacks. The categories come from an AI R&D task taxonomy developed by Epoch AI.

OpenAI also cautions that researchers may have changed which tasks they hand to agents as the tools improved, so the rise could reflect both better models and different task choices.

## Heavy and growing use

Valued at API list prices, the median researcher's daily agent usage reached about $2,400 in October, up 62% from September; at the 90th percentile it hit about $23,000. In a survey, the median researcher said they would give up 30% of team compute, or separately 40% of team headcount, to keep frontier coding agents for three months.

The company says documented effort on safety and alignment work grew 77% from January to September, the largest absolute increase of any major research area.

## Why OpenAI is publishing this

OpenAI says AI labs should be required to track and publicly report indicators of recursive self-improvement, where AI speeds up the development of future AI, and that humans remain central to its research loop. As Data Phoenix notes, these are internal estimates that OpenAI has not released the underlying data for, so they can't be independently reproduced.
