The European Commission has confirmed that it was hit by a cyberattack last month that relied on artificial intelligence. A Commission spokesperson, Thomas Regnier, told journalists on Thursday that the first signs of the intrusion were detected on October 22 and described it as an "AI-generated cyber campaign," The Record reports.

## What was hit

The attack reportedly disrupted the Commission's personnel systems for more than 10 days, affecting payroll, leave requests, medical reimbursements and translation services. The Commission said it took several services offline as a protective measure, notified affected people, and is checking other systems while working with CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor.

Regnier said investigators had found no evidence of a broad breach of staff data and knew of no ransom demand. The investigation is still open. Asked why the incident was not disclosed earlier, he said publicizing defensive steps could make them less effective.

## The tool in question

The Commission did not name the tool or the attackers. POLITICO, which first reported the incident, said CERT-EU had identified ARTEX, an open-source AI hacking framework released in July that uses commercial AI models to automate finding and exploiting vulnerabilities. The FBI warned last week that ARTEX was being used by both criminals and state-backed groups, with some intrusions going from first targeting to privileged access in under three hours, according to The Record.

This is the Commission's third disclosed cyber incident this year, after breaches involving Ivanti software in January and its cloud infrastructure in March. Officials have not linked the three.
