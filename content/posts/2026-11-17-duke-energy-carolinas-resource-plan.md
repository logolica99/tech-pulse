The North Carolina Utilities Commission (NCUC) has approved Duke Energy's 2025 Carolinas Resource Plan, the utility's long-range plan for meeting fast-growing electricity demand in North and South Carolina. Duke announced the order on 17 November and said it was issued the day before. Duke serves about 4.8 million customers in the two states.

## What the order advances

These additions are on top of resources approved in the commission's 2022 and 2024 orders. Duke says some projects still need their own reviews and approvals.

- **Solar:** 2,300 MW of new solar, for 8,100 MW in total by 2034.
- **Batteries:** 2,700 MW of new storage, for 5,400 MW in total by 2034.
- **Gas combined cycle:** two more units totaling 2,720 MW, for five units by 2033.
- **Gas combustion turbines:** three more units totaling 1,111 MW, for seven by 2032, including turbines at the former Buck coal plant site.

## Nuclear

The order lets Duke keep developing either small modular reactors or large light-water reactors. Duke can prepare and submit Nuclear Regulatory Commission amendments for two Westinghouse AP1000 units at its W.S. Lee site in South Carolina, and revisions to its combined license application for two AP1000 units at the Shearon Harris site in North Carolina. The commission also opened a new docket where Duke will post quarterly updates on nuclear progress, so regulators can weigh technology, cost, financing and execution risk before any decision to build.

## Costs

Duke says the plan's projected cost to customers over the next decade is nearly half that of the plan approved in 2024, because it was adjusted for state and federal policy changes. The company also points to an estimated $2.7 billion in net nuclear and solar tax credits between 2025 and 2028, and $2.3 billion in projected savings from 2027 to 2040 from combining Duke Energy Carolinas and Duke Energy Progress on 1 January.
