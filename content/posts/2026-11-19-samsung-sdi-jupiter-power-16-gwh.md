Samsung SDI has signed a 16 GWh deal to supply lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries to Jupiter Power, a US developer that builds, owns and operates large battery storage projects. The Korean battery maker announced the agreement on Nov. 16, the first day of the RE+ clean energy trade show in Las Vegas.

## The deal

The contract runs for three years starting in 2029. Samsung SDI put its value at an "estimated several trillion Korean Won." The batteries will be made in Indiana, where Samsung SDI has two plants: one it now owns outright after buying out General Motors' share of their joint venture, and another run as a joint venture with Stellantis.

Samsung SDI is converting electric vehicle battery lines in the US into LFP lines for grid storage that meet domestic-content rules. The company said the new order brings its total booked storage orders from Jupiter Power to about 27 GWh. In its third-quarter results, it reported 60 GWh of energy storage battery orders in the first nine months of 2026, and earlier said its US LFP production had reached 30 GWh of annual capacity.

## The buyer

Jupiter Power is owned by Global Infrastructure Partners, part of BlackRock. According to Energy-Storage.News, the developer raised $1.4 billion a few months ago to back 10 storage projects in Texas and Michigan totaling 1,500 MW and 3,600 MWh.
