New York has put a five-year pause on toys that talk to children through AI chatbots. Governor Kathy Hochul signed S.9408A/A.11144B, which bars the manufacture, distribution and sale in the state of toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots, such as talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with kids.

## What the law does

- The moratorium lasts five years.
- It takes effect 90 days after signing, so it will not apply to this holiday season. Hochul urged parents to weigh the risks before buying such toys as gifts.
- Violators face civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day for each violation, enforced by the state Attorney General. Attorney General Letitia James said her office "will not hesitate to enforce" it.

## Why

The governor's office pointed to testing by a national consumer watchdog of chatbot toys marketed to children aged 3 to 12. Some toys talked in depth about sexually explicit topics, told kids where to find matches at home and acted upset when a child had to leave. Many had few or no parental controls, their safety limits weakened over longer conversations, and some could record a child's voice or scan their face. The Guardian also cites an NBC News test in which a plush toy marketed to children as young as three discussed violent topics.

The bill's sponsors, State Senator Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay, framed the pause as time to study the toys' effects on children and write proper rules. The Guardian notes Hochul has also placed a one-year moratorium on AI data center construction in the state.
