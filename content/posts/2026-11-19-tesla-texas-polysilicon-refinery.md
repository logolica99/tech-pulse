Tesla is looking at moving further up the solar supply chain. An application it filed under Texas's Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation (JETI) incentive program describes a $3.1 billion plant in Nueces County, near Robstown, that would refine high-purity polysilicon, the raw material that gets turned into silicon ingots and solar cells.

## What the filing says

- **Site:** land near State Highway 44 and FM 1694, inside the Tuloso-Midway school district.
- **Spending:** an Ernst & Young analysis included with the application budgets about $1.5 billion for engineering and construction and roughly $1.4 billion for equipment, with construction running from 2027 through 2030.
- **Jobs:** 260 people in 2030, 385 in 2031 and 515 in 2032, at an average of about $130,000 a year including benefits, according to the EY projection.
- **Tax break:** Tesla is asking for relief on the school district's operations taxes, with qualifying property exempt from 2028 through 2030 and partially taxed through 2040.

The application does not say how much polysilicon the plant would produce or how much water it would use.

## Not a done deal

This is an application, not an executed agreement. Tesla says in the filing that it is also considering sites outside Texas, and that without the incentive it would not make the investment in the state.

The refinery would sit upstream of Tesla's other announced solar plans, including the proposed Crystal Sun cell and module factory in Fort Bend County, Texas, and expanded module production in Buffalo, New York, as reported by TeslaNorth.
