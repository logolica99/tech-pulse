Voyager 1 has passed a mile marker no other human-made object has reached: one light-day from Earth. NASA timed the crossing for Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026, at 5:16:07 a.m. EST (2:16 a.m. PT), 49 years, two months, and 11 days after launch.

## How far that is

A light-day is the distance light covers in 24 hours. NASA puts it at 16,094,799,096 miles, or about 25.9 billion kilometers. For comparison, light from the Sun reaches Earth in about eight minutes and 20 seconds.

In practical terms, it means a command sent from Earth now takes a full day to reach the spacecraft, and its reply takes another day to come back.

## Still moving

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 became the first spacecraft to enter interstellar space in 2012. NASA says it is cruising at nearly 38,000 mph and heading toward the star Gliese 445, which it should pass within about 1.7 light-years of in roughly 40,000 years.

Its twin, Voyager 2, took a slower route past Uranus and Neptune and is on pace to reach its own one-light-day mark in November 2035.
