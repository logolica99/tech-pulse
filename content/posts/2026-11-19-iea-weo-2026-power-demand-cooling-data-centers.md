The International Energy Agency published its World Energy Outlook 2026 on Nov. 19, and it expects the world to need a lot more electricity than it thought a year ago. In its main scenario, which counts policies already in place plus those governments have formally proposed, global demand climbs from 28,576 TWh in 2025 to 39,042 TWh in 2035, a rise of about 36.6%. The 2035 figure is 1,300 TWh (3%) higher than last year's outlook.

## Cooling is the biggest new load

The IEA raised its forecast for household air conditioner ownership after recent heatwaves. Cooling and appliances together add 2,900 TWh by 2035, more than the European Union uses today, and air conditioning alone adds about 1,700 TWh. The agency says demand for cooling in buildings is now overtaking demand for heating for the first time. Because air conditioning use is so seasonal, cooling is projected to make up 12% of electricity use in 2035 but 30% of peak demand.

The report also points to a wider push to electrify after this year's disruption to oil and gas shipping through the Strait of Hormuz; according to Eco-Business, 25 countries have announced new electrification policies since February.

## Data centers double by 2030

The IEA now expects more data centers to be built, especially in the United States. Data center electricity use doubles from about 500 TWh today to 1,000 TWh by 2030 and reaches 1,500 TWh by 2035, with AI servers driving about three-quarters of the growth to 2030. China, the EU and the US account for more than 80% of that demand.

Data centers still use only about 1.7% of the world's electricity, and by 2035 they add less new demand than electric vehicles or factories making batteries and solar panels. But the load is concentrated, coal and gas supply about 60% of it today, and the sector's emissions are on track to rise 70% by 2030. The report says that while AI could help optimize power systems, surging data center demand is for now adding strain to some markets and supply chains.

## Fast growth in Asia

Electricity demand in Southeast Asia is projected to grow about 5% a year, from 1,369 TWh in 2025 to 2,197 TWh in 2035. Together, Southeast Asia and India add 2,100 TWh over the decade as incomes rise and more households buy appliances and air conditioners.
