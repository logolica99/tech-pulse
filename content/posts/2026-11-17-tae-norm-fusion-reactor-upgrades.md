TAE Technologies has shared progress on a set of upgrades to Norm, its sixth-generation fusion reactor. The goal, according to the company's announcement, is to push the machine from the 70 million degrees Celsius it has reached so far to 100 million degrees, the temperature threshold for deuterium-tritium fusion.

Norm is notable for how it makes plasma. TAE says it was the first reactor to form, heat and sustain a field-reversed configuration (FRC) plasma using only particle accelerator beams, which the company argues makes for a simpler and cheaper design.

## What is being upgraded

TAE describes three workstreams aimed at keeping plasma hot, dense and stable at the same time:

- **Fueling:** better control over fuel, for denser and longer-lasting plasma.
- **Neutral beams:** higher-power beams that add both heat and fuel.
- **Radio-frequency heating:** extra heat without adding more fuel.

The company is targeting 100 million °C at fusion-relevant density and confinement in 2027, though it notes that depends on installation and testing. Hitting those conditions in a linear machine would, in TAE's view, show that the plasma regime needed for commercial fusion can be reached in a compact, lower-cost design, and set up its longer-term goal of running on hydrogen-boron fuel.

## The road to Da Vinci

Norm is the test bed for Da Vinci, a planned 50 MW plant meant to validate the physics of TAE's approach. The new fueling, beam and heating systems are the same ones Da Vinci is expected to run on. TAE is targeting a fusion energy demonstration at Da Vinci by 2031.
