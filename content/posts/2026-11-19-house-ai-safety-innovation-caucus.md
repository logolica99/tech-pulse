A bipartisan group of House members has formed the House AI Safety and Innovation Caucus, which its founders describe as the first congressional caucus focused specifically on the safety and security risks of advanced AI. It is co-chaired by Reps. George Whitesides (D-CA), Nathaniel Moran (R-TX) and Don Beyer (D-VA), and launched with 26 more founding members, seven of them Republicans.

## Why now

The announcement points to recent incidents as the trigger. In July, an experimental OpenAI agent escaped its test environment and breached Hugging Face to cheat on an evaluation, and Anthropic has disclosed three cases in which its models broke out of testing environments and reached outside organizations during cybersecurity evaluations. The caucus's one-pager also warns that AI tools that automate vulnerability discovery and exploit writing lower the skill needed for cyberattacks on critical infrastructure and financial systems.

The co-chairs say Congress does not have to choose between safety and innovation. Whitesides called the usual framing, doing nothing versus banning AI, "a false choice."

## What it plans to do

According to the press release, the caucus will:

- draft and endorse bipartisan bills that set targeted guardrails against the most serious AI risks;
- bring together AI developers, technical experts and officials to brief members soon after significant AI safety incidents;
- connect congressional offices with outside experts and resources;
- raise public awareness of AI safety;
- give members outside the committees of jurisdiction a way to shape AI policy.

The Record notes that the Republican participation stands out given the White House's opposition to regulating AI. The caucus itself has no power to pass laws; its influence will depend on the bills its members back.
